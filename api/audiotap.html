    Detailed Description

    The client (ravving-video, stereo inputs, no outputs) copies its inputs into a ring of 2^20 frames (about 21 s at 48 kHz) on the audio thread; a plain thread of its own drains the ring into a named pipe that ffmpeg reads as raw audio (32-bit float, interleaved stereo, the graph's rate). What the ring cannot hold is counted (droppedFrames()) and reported, never lost silently.

    It does not use AudioLevels because libzynthbox runs one AudioLevels recording at a time, which must stay free for MixRecorder and InputRecorder; nor ffmpeg's own JACK input, whose small ring a stall of the encoder or the storage would overrun.

    The audio thread touches atomics and the preallocated ring only. The drain thread wakes every few milliseconds, writes what is there, and polls the pipe before each write, so an ffmpeg that stopped reading cannot hang stopCapture().

    A microphone can be mixed in: two more inputs (mic_l, mic_r, micPorts()) that the live recording's input path feeds (LiveRecMic::setListener()) while setMic(true). The audio thread adds them to the captured sound only; the speakers never get it. While the mic is in, the audio thread also meters it (takeMicPeak()) and reports whether the voice-over is ducking the music (voiceActive()).

    Time: the first captured sample's wall-clock moment (startRealtimeUs(), CLOCK_REALTIME in microseconds) is taken on the audio thread from the cycle's start time (jack_get_cycle_times()), so the recorder can tell ffmpeg where the audio sits against the video frames, which x11grab and v4l2 stamp with the same clock.

    Members are called on the GUI thread unless they say otherwise.

    void AudioTap::setVoiceOver(float duckDb, float liftDb)

    Sets the voice-over mix of the mic: while the mic carries a voice the master is turned down by duckDb dB (0: not at all) and the mic is lifted by liftDb dB and made crisp (a 120 Hz high-pass, a +4 dB shelf above 3 kHz). A voice is the mic above a threshold that follows the music: at least -34 dBFS, and 12 dB over what the speakers are heard to leak into the mic (learned as the mic's level over the master's: down fast, up slowly, capped at -26 dB of the master), so a beat the mic hears does not duck itself. Both 0 give the plain sum. Either way, with the mic in, the sum goes through a look-ahead peak limiter (1 ms ahead, ceiling -1 dBFS, 150 ms release), so a hot mic over a loud beat is turned down before the peak arrives instead of clipping.

    bool AudioTap::startCapture(const QString &fifoPath)

    Starts capturing into the named pipe fifoPath, made when missing: from the next audio cycle on, samples go to the ring, and the drain thread feeds the pipe once its reader opens it. captureStarted() follows with the first sample's wall-clock time. Returns false when the client is not valid, a capture is already running, or the pipe cannot be made.

    qint64 AudioTap::startRealtimeUs() const

    Returns the first captured sample's CLOCK_REALTIME time in microseconds; 0 before it is known.

    void AudioTap::stopCapture()

    Stops capturing: the drain thread writes what is left (giving a reader that stopped reading at most a second) and closes the pipe, which ends ffmpeg's audio input. captureEnded() follows.

    float AudioTap::takeMicPeak()

    Returns the mic's own peak (absolute sample value, before the lift) since the last call, and starts the next one at 0.

    bool AudioTap::voiceActive() const

    Returns whether the voice over is ducking the music at this moment.
