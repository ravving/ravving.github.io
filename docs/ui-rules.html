    UI rules

    The shared base types, action against state buttons, one ✕ to leave, readable palettes.

    ravving's screens share one look:

    • machines with side rails and a face in their own skin;
    • controls grouped on strips;
    • work areas recessed in wells;
    • a dark header band on top.

    These rules keep every new screen in it. tools/check-ui.py checks them over the QML, as a laptop test (ctest -R ui-rules) and in tools/static-analysis.sh ui. tools/ui-rules.json holds the rules and their exceptions, and is where a new rule goes.

    Where a control lives

    Three layers, each built on the one below; a layer never reaches up.

    Layer Holds Example
    Ravving.Style (style/) a Qt Quick Control that only needs ravving's look Button, Slider, TextField, Switch, Frame, ToolBar, Page
    Ravving.Controls (controls/) a control with properties or behaviour of its own, besides the look ActionButton, Chip, ConfirmButton, Knob, Fader, Meter, KeyCap (on CapFace, the grid's cap the SONG page's keys and blocks share), Nameplate
    the app (qml/) the screens, built from the two above the pages, the panels, the dialogs

    The style adds no properties to a control: only attached ones (Skin). A control that needs properties of its own goes into the kit.

    The kit can't reach the style's private parts, so two faces exist in both layers and change together. A Chip's key (the dark key, the face 1 px in and 5 px short, its top highlight) is the style's KeySurface. FocusRing is the style's ButtonFocus ring. A change to one is made to the other.

    Both read Ravving.Theme (theme/), the tokens; the kit also answers the device's keys and knobs (Ravving.Input, input/). A screen uses the kit's control rather than redoing what it does.

    A kit control knows nothing of the app. What it needs of the app's state comes in as a property, or through Interaction: the modal layers up (the kit keeps them), the knobs' kind and the page-focus request (Main.qml wires those).

    Shared base types

    Use the component; never redraw it.

    Buttons: action or state

    • ActionButton: it does something once and leaves no state. LOAD, CLEAR, SAVE, RENAME, NEW, −/+, ◀ ▶, PLAY FROM HERE, TRIM, TO PADS, the transport keys, a picker showing its current value (it opens a list).
    • One highlighted action at most on a page: its main one, drawn as the webpage's flat orange face (design-system B22), as the setup page's TRY IT. Every other action keeps the key.
    • Chip / StripButton: a state you can see. It stays lit while the state is on, so it always binds checked, or is checkable. Checked: chip-shows-state.
    • ConfirmButton for what cannot be undone: the first tap arms it (AGAIN? and a countdown bar), a second one within three seconds does it.
    • All of them at the standard size (see Button sizes). Checked: actions-are-actionbuttons (no bare QQC.Button).

    How a chip looks

    A chip is told from a button by its face, never by its size or corners (the same, see Corners and Button sizes):

    • A button is a raised key (the style's face): a lighter top, a highlight, a dark lip.
    • A chip is a switch: raised as a button is (its frame, lip and top highlight) on its own translucent face. On, or pressed, it sits flush and backlit as the project plate on the SONG page (RimGlow): the rim lit, the light spilling round it, the face tinted.
    • A toggle and a choice of a set look the same; what is on is lit. One shown as a glyph (Chip.iconName alone: LOCK's padlock, closed or open) says its state with the glyph too.

    Corners

    Two radii, from the theme, so everything that is alike looks alike:

    • Theme.radius (4): every control. Buttons, chips, strip buttons, fields, the step keys, pads and section keys, the cells of a grid. A layer drawn inside one (a hover, a focus ring, an inner gloss) takes Theme.radius - 1.
    • Theme.surfaceRadius (6): every surface. Pages, the machines' panes, plates and nameplates, wells (QQC.Frame), strips, cards and list rows, toasts, and the dialogs (the popup's card) the same as the panes.
    • Round things are drawn round, from their size: a dot or a knob width / 2, a progress bar or a meter height / 2. A radius of 2 or less (a hairline, a tick) may stay a number.

    Checked: radius-from-theme (no other number as a radius in QML).

    Groups

    • Controls that belong together sit on one ControlGroup: − OCT +, ↶ ↷, ‹ 1/1 ›, a section's or a part's ◀ ▶. Its well reaches 2 px past them, so the row keeps its size, and its corners are the surfaces' (the controls' sit concentric inside).
    • A knob that drives a group wears its badge on the group's corner, never between its controls. A caption names the group from outside its well (LENGTH, MOVE, REPEATS).

    Button sizes

    • One height: Rave.controlHeight (42 px) for every button, action or state, so a row of them lines up and a finger always finds the same target on the 1280×800 touch screen.
    • A smallest width: Rave.controlWidth (48 px, square) for a glyph (◀ ▶ − + ⟳ ✕), and Rave.actionMinWidth (88 px) for a word (LOAD, SCAN, SAVE). A short word is no smaller a target than a long one, and a longer label grows past it.
    • ActionButton, Chip and StripButton apply both on their own (minimumWidth): a chip is exactly a button's size. A button may ask for more, never less: the dialogs' main action, SAVE at 150, or SCAN at 130 so SCANNING… doesn't shift the row.
    • A row that wants bigger buttons sizes them all from one property, never with numbers on each button. Checked: button-standard-size (no number under the standard on a button's height or width).

    Controls in a group take the device knobs

    • Dials, switches (WaveCycler) and sliders that sit together are a KnobGroup, with them as its targets.
    • The group takes the focus: a tap on it, or ◀ ▶ between groups.
    • The device's three small knobs then drive its controls in order, and the big knob one more.
    • Each control wears its KnobBadge (1, 2, 3) saying which knob moves it, with the pick-up diamond on the fixed-pot kit.
    • A group shows its frame while it has the knobs.
    • The models: ParamCard (the MODS and SOUND EFFECTS cards), and the header's TEMPO, FX SENDS and MASTER.
    • So nothing a finger can turn is out of reach of the hardware, and the badges say which knob is which without a manual. Checked: grouped-controls-take-knobs (a file with dials, switches or sliders and no KnobGroup).

    Rows of controls sit on bands (dialogs and pages)

    • A page's or a dialog's row of controls sits on one strip (a QQC.ToolBar): the darker rounded band a pattern toolbar is, with the preset, OCT and undo / redo on one band.
    • One row, one continuous band, never split into a band per group.
    • The groups in it (a Caption first, then its chips) are told apart by the space between them, as the toolbar's are.
    • In the presets and sounds dialogs (ChoicePopup), SOURCE at the left and LOAD at the right share their row's band. GROUP and SORT share theirs: the groups wrap in what SORT leaves them, and SORT keeps the top right.
    • A row of one group hugs it: SAVE PATTERN AS's KEEP, NamePopup's options under optionsLabel.
    • A page's toolbar rows too: the SONG page's top bar (its title, SWITCH, PLAYS), its section and song rows, the pattern toolbars.
    • So a dialog reads in rows and groups at a glance, as a page does, and a wrapping row never leaves a lone chip on a line of its own. Checked: dialog-groups-on-bands (a Caption in a *Popup.qml outside a strip); one band per row is the reviewer's to see.

    Leaving

    • One ✕, the CloseButton, everywhere. A dialog gets it from DialogHeader; a full page puts it at the end of its header's first row. Checked: close-is-closebutton.
    • A view under the header has no ✕ (the SONG page, the MIXER): the header stays on screen, and the control that opened the view closes it (the project plate, ✱), as BACK does.
    • No CLOSE / CANCEL / DONE buttons: the ✕ and BACK already leave. A button that stops work under way (CANCEL an export) is an action, and a question's answers (NO / YES while pairing) are answers; both are listed as exceptions. Checked: no-close-text.

    Pages

    • Every page wears the chassis in its own skin: a QQC.Page (or the QQC.Pane of a machine's module). Checked: page-wears-chassis.
    • A page that takes the whole window (MUSIC PLAYER, RECORDER) has a header band like the dashboard's: a QQC.ToolBar with position: QQC.ToolBar.Header. Checked: full-page-has-header.
    • Row 1: its transport, a Plate saying what's on, its own toggles, and the ✕.
    • Row 2: its deck of grouped controls, on strips (QQC.ToolBar).
    • The view sits under it, on its QQC.Page.
    • Its controls go on strips by group, its work areas in wells (QQC.Frame).

    Empty views

    An empty list or view says why and what fills it, with an EmptyNote ("no recordings yet · ● REC records the input as played"), never a Text of its own. Where the view has room, ARU stands above the words in the note's ink; mascot: "" leaves ARU out. Checked: empty-says-why.

    Scrolling views

    A view that can be taller (or wider) than its room carries the style's ScrollIndicator (QQC.ScrollIndicator.vertical: QQC.ScrollIndicator {}). It is a 3 px pill along the edge, there whenever the content doesn't fit, faint at rest and brighter while it moves. A finger scrolls the view itself; the indicator only says there is more.

    The type scale

    Every text takes one of eleven steps from Theme.type, family, size, weight, tracking and case in one: font: Theme.type.label. Set the whole font, not font.pixelSize beside it. The values come from theme/tokens.json type.steps (ctest design-tokens holds them equal).

    Step Face Size For
    hero, title, heading, plate display, capitals 40, 28, 22, 15 the splash, titles, a card's heading or a value bubble, nameplates and dialog titles
    lede, body, label, detail regular 15, 13, 12, 11 a line under a title, running text, a control's caption, a secondary line under a title (who made it, a card's detail, a hint)
    caps, micro monospaced, capitals 11, 9 group titles, tags and hints; a sub-label on a plate or a key, nothing smaller
    figure monospaced 13 a value that changes while watched

    A text with no step of its own takes the application font, body's size.

    Controls, emphasis and symbols

    Buttons and combo boxes look alike: the style gives both the label step and the same button background, and so do the kit's ActionButton, Chip and StripButton. A control's caption doesn't set its own size; a long translation shrinks to fit instead.

    Setting one part of a control's font (font.pixelSize, font.bold) replaces the style's whole font and loses the step's weight. A control that needs another step sets the whole one: font: Theme.type.caps.

    Emphasis keeps the step: font: Theme.type.strong(Theme.type.label) is one weight bolder, and Theme.type.italic(Theme.type.detail) is slanted.

    Icons

    A symbol is never a font character. Play, stop, record, undo, the arrows, close and the check are ravving's own pixel icons (assets/icons, written by tools/icons/make-icons.py).

    • The set: the one SETTINGS · LOOK · ICONS picks (Theme.iconSet: candy, block, hairline or a user's icon pack, IconPacks; Make an icon pack). A new icon's name goes into tools/icons/make-pack.py and that page too (ctest icons-current).
    • On a button: iconName (ActionButton, ConfirmButton, Chip, StripButton) or icon.source: Interaction.icon(name). Anything else shows an Icon.
    • Sizes: 16 beside words and 24 on a key, so every set draws at a whole scale and no pixel blurs.
    • In a hint or notice that names the device's keys, the string keeps the characters (▲ ▼ ◀ ▶ ■ ● ✱ → ✓) for its translators, and Interaction.withIcons() draws each one as its icon.

    Literal sizes

    Drawings sized on purpose are the only exceptions: the big readout and the tuner's note. The files that still set a literal pixelSize (or a control's fontSize) are listed with how many they have. That number only goes down: a new literal fails, and so does a count left higher than the file needs. Checked: font-sizes-are-steps.

    Three font families

    ravving's text uses three families, all built in (the Fonts class), each with one job:

    Role Family Used for In QML
    regular IBM Plex Sans every label, caption and name the application's font: nothing to set
    monospaced IBM Plex Mono figures that change while watched Rave.monospaceFamily
    display Chakra Petch (SemiBold) branding: the instruments' nameplates Rave.displayFamily

    A value you change while watching it is monospaced: every digit is as wide as the next, so a turning value doesn't make its label jitter or move its neighbours. That covers:

    • the dials' readouts (Knob's valueText): the MODS and effects knobs, the master and input levels in dB, pan, semitones;
    • the sliders' readouts (Fader, LevelSlider);
    • counters and times.

    The display face is ravving's own, also on the webpage's headings. It is built in at one weight (SemiBold) with no italic, so it is used as it is. No file names a family in place, so changing one is a single line in theme/fonts.cpp. Checked: font-families.

    Palettes

    Every page has a skin of its own in Rave.qml (Rave.machineSkins, songSkin, playerSkin, recorderSkins): chassisTop, chassisBottom, rail, well, ink, dim, accent, glow, dark.

    A new one:

    • Starts from a researched palette, not a guess. Pick its family from a reference palette set: the Radix, Tailwind or Material colour scales, Adobe Color's or Coolors' tested schemes, or a brand's own. Then map its steps:
    Colour Step
    chassis 950–900, dark
    well one step darker than the chassis
    rail a mid step of the same hue
    ink 50
    dim 400
    accent 400–500, or its complement for contrast
    glow a lighter step of the accent
    - Is readable (WCAG 2.x contrast), against both chassis colours. Checked:
    palette-readable, which prints the ratios when it fails.
    - ink ≥ 7:1 (AAA body text);
    - dim ≥ 4.5:1 (AA text);
    - accent ≥ 3:1 (AA for non-text), on the chassis or the well it lights in.
    - Is distinct from the pages beside it: the player is violet, the RECORDER's inputs wine, amber
    and teal, the SONG page a matte slate night.

    Skin sets

    A skin set (data/skins/<set>.json, SETTINGS → LOOK → SKINS) gives every machine its case, keys, well and lights. The shipped sets: ARCADE, CEL, JELLY, KISSA, METRO, PENCIL (the default), PREVIOUS, REEF, SIGNAL, SORBET, STUDIO and TOON, beside ravving's own (Rave.ownSkins). A profile naming a set that is not there wears PENCIL.

    python3 tools/check-skins.py [FILE.json…] checks each colour against what it is drawn on (all the shipped sets when no file is given):

    • dark matches the case's luminance (Rave.isDarkColour);
    • ink ≥ 7:1 on both chassis colours;
    • ink ≥ 4.5:1 on the key caps where the machine draws them (the step grids of the drums and both samplers, the chords' keys): cap, capShade, groupCap, groupCapShade;
    • what is written on the well ≥ 4.5:1 on it: the well's ink and its quiet labels (below);
    • glow ≥ 3:1 on those caps and on the well: a lit key, a lit step, a note or chord in a roll;
    • accent ≥ 3:1 on the chassis or on the well: lit chips, knob arcs, custom chords;
    • glow ≥ 3:1 on the header's instrument chip, plateColor ≥ 4.5:1 on the nameplate;
    • every two machines' glows apart (Delta E ≥ 20), so the header's lights tell them apart.

    Fixing and comparing sets

    tools/skin-fix.py FILE.json… nudges a failing set until it passes, moving only the colours a check names and only their lightness. tools/check-skins.py --similar lists the sets closest to each other: a new set should stay at least 16 from every other (the mean Delta E of the machines' cases, keys, wells and lights).

    Light and dark cases

    Any machine may wear a light case or a dark one, with a light well or a dark one. The views on the well take what reads on it from WellColours (after Rave.onWell):

    • the views: the drums' step grid, the chords' roll and slots, the bass and lead rolls, the lead's ribbon and the pad grid, so every page can wear a light case;
    • on a dark well: the light greys and white washes;
    • on a light well: the same marks in the well's dark ink;
    • the controls in them: that ink, through Skin.overrides;
    • the tabs over them (TabButton): the case's ink.

    A light case's lights are mid tones, to read on its near-white caps and in the header's black chip alike.

    The skin

    A page's root, or a machine's module, sets its skin as Skin.skin (readonly property var skin: Rave.… and Skin.skin: page.skin). It reaches every control under it and the dialogs the page declares. With no skin set, a control keeps its own default.

    • A kit control given no colour takes the skin's accent and ink.
    • A style control takes only its accent, for what it draws on a face of its own.
    • The style's surfaces take their parts: the chassis (Page, Pane) its rail and gradient, a well (Frame) its well, a strip (ToolBar) its darkness, a separator its ink.
    • A plain Text on the page reads Skin.ink or Skin.dim.

    Pass a colour only where it differs from the skin. A surface with a colour of its own, like the dialog card or a dark band on a light page, keeps the accent and sets its own ink with Skin.overrides. The dialog card sets its own well too (Theme.colors.wellSurface). Checked: qml-health's skin family.

    Design tokens

    theme/tokens.json holds every shared colour, family and shape in one place. Its groups:

    Group Holds
    core the theme's colours, each naming its Theme.colors property and the webpage's custom property
    site the webpage's own tones, and its light set (site.light: the paper colours, what the art keeps, the contrast pairs checked)
    fonts the three families
    skins the skins, as in Rave.qml
    aru the mascot's colours
    art the cover art's palette: the skins' accents through the covers' fade
    type the type scale: Theme.type's steps, and the --t-<step> sizes of the webpage (site/site.css) and the API reference (doc/global/style/ravving.css)

    A colour changes there first, then in the file it names: theme/theme.h, site/site.css, Rave.qml or data/branding/make-aru.py. python3 tools/design-tokens.py --write recomputes the cover art's palette after a skin changes and writes the type scale into both stylesheets; --sheet OUT.html renders the tokens as a page. Checked: design-tokens.

    A key and its button

    A device key that does what an on-screen button does shows that button pressed while it is held, as PLAY does the header's ▶ and BACK the toolbar's ↶ on a grid (down: pressed || ravving.heldKeys…).

    A key means one thing per place. Two parts of a screen shown together don't both bind it. No page binds a key the input layer takes first: ALT + MODE, ALT + ▲, ALT + 1, X, Y, ALT + the step keys and the knobs. Checked: tools/check-shortcuts.py.

    Text is translatable, keys are not

    Every text a screen shows goes through i18nc, with a context (Translations).

    • A key that's stored or compared stays English, and is translated where it's drawn: the Rave display helpers, with Rave.kli18nc to mark it.
    • The device's printed key names and the product names stay as printed.
    • Translations run longer, Spanish by about a third. ActionButton and Chip shrink their text to fit, down to 75 %, so give words room and look at every new screen in Spanish.

    Checked: text-translatable, a text: given a literal string without i18n…, outside the keep list in tools/ui-rules.json.

    Adding a rule

    1. Write it here, under its heading, with why.
    2. Add it to tools/ui-rules.json: a pattern (a regex no QML may match) or a check (a function in tools/check-ui.py, registered in CHECKS), with the files allowed to break it and why. A known breach still to fix is allowed with "migrate": true: it shows as a warning, and the list only ever shrinks.
    3. Run python3 tools/check-ui.py --verbose; fix what it finds or list it.
