    Make a skin set

    The colours of the eight instrument pages as one JSON file: every key, the contrast rules, a checker to download, and how to put the set on a MIKO.

    A skin set gives every instrument page in ravving its colours: the case, the keys, the grid's inset and the lights. It is one JSON file. Drop it on the device and it shows up in SETTINGS → DEVICE → SKINS by its name, with no restart.

    This page is all you need to make one, with or without ravving's source: the file format, what each colour paints, the rules a set must pass, a checker to run them, and how to put the set on a MIKO.

    Downloads

    • check-skins.py: the checker. Python 3, nothing to install.
    • Sets to start from: pencil.json, previous.json, studio.json, metro.json.

    The screen it paints

    The MIKO's screen is 1280 × 800. A fixed dark graphite header sits on top of every page: the transport, the LIVE FX pads, and a chip per instrument lit in that instrument's glow. Under it, the page of the instrument on screen, in its skin:

    Machine key Page What sits on its well
    drums1, drums2, drums3 the three drum machines a step grid: 16 key caps per row, nine rows, every other group of four in groupCap
    chords CHORDS a chord roll (rows of chords, notes as bars), the SLOTS view, and seven chord keys under it on cap
    bass, lead BASS, LEAD a piano roll: note rows, notes as lit cells
    sampler SLOT SAMPLER slots, a waveform, a step grid of caps
    pads PAD SAMPLER 16 pads, a waveform, a step lane of caps

    The player, the RECORDER and the SONG page keep their own colours: a set changes the eight machines only.

    The file

    {
    "ravving": "preset",
    "type": "skin",
    "skin": {
        "name": "PENCIL",
        "about": "a pencil case: kraft, graph and lined paper, a chalkboard, a red case and highlighters",
        "machines": {
            "drums1": {"plateColor": "#fea292", "dark": false, "chassisTop": "#fee7cd", "chassisBottom": "#f0d4b5", "ink": "#281d0f", "accent": "#c13425", "glow": "#cf4a3a", "cap": "#fef5eb", "capShade": "#edd5ba", "capBorder": "#7d6446", "groupCap": "#fee4e0", "groupCapShade": "#fac6bd", "well": "#edd0b0", "rail": "#76382e"},
            "drums2": {"...": "the same fourteen keys"},
            "drums3": {}, "chords": {}, "bass": {}, "lead": {}, "sampler": {}, "pads": {}
        }
    }
}
    • "ravving": "preset" and "type": "skin" are required, or the file is ignored.
    • name is the button in SKINS (capitals read best); about is the line under the swatches.
    • The file name, without .json, is the set's key: lowercase letters and digits, no spaces (harbour.json). A file named like a shipped set replaces it on that device.
    • A machine left out, or a key left out of a machine, keeps ravving's own colour. Give all eight machines all fourteen keys, so the set looks as you made it.
    • Colours are #rrggbb. No alpha, no names.

    The keys

    Key What it paints
    dark true for a dark case, false for a light one. It picks the ink of everything drawn on the case, so it must match the case (the checker says when it doesn't).
    chassisTop, chassisBottom the page's case: a vertical gradient from top to bottom. Keep the two close.
    rail the case's end cheeks, left and right of the page, and the rails of strips.
    ink text and icons on the case and on the keys: labels, names, values.
    accent what's on: lit chips and buttons, knob arcs, custom chords.
    glow the lights: a lit step, an LED, a note or chord in a roll, the header's chip. Usually a lighter step of accent.
    plateColor the instrument's name on its nameplate (a black plate), drawn as neon.
    cap, capShade a key cap: its face, and the shade at its foot.
    capBorder the keys' outline.
    groupCap, groupCapShade the caps of every other group of four steps, so a bar reads as 4 + 4 + 4 + 4. Same lightness as cap, a small shift in hue.
    well the inset the grids and rolls sit in.

    Light cases and dark wells

    Any machine may wear a light case or a dark one, with a light well or a dark one. The marks on the well (grid lines, row names, empty cells) take their colour from the well, not the case:

    • a well as dark as the case (or as light) uses the machine's ink;
    • a dark well in a light case uses near white #f4f4f6; a light well in a dark case uses near black #16161a;
    • quiet labels on the well: #a0a0a8 on a dark well, the ink at 70 % on a light one.

    A light case's lights must be mid tones: they have to read on its near-white caps and in the header's black chip alike.

    The rules

    Contrast is WCAG 2.x (relative luminance; ratios from 1:1 to 21:1). A set passes when every machine passes all of these, and the checker reports nothing:

    Rule Minimum
    dark matches the case: the mean of chassisTop and chassisBottom is dark when its Rec. 709 luma (0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B, channels 0–1, not linearised) is under 0.5 must match
    ink on chassisTop and on chassisBottom 7:1
    ink on the caps, where the machine has caps (drums, both samplers: cap, capShade, groupCap, groupCapShade; chords: cap, capShade) 4.5:1
    the well's ink and its quiet labels (above) on well 4.5:1
    glow on those caps and on well: a lit step, a lit key, a note in a roll 3:1
    accent on the case (both chassis colours) or on well, whichever is higher 3:1
    glow on the header's chip, #0f0f12 3:1
    plateColor on the nameplate, #1b1b20 4.5:1
    every two machines' glows apart (CIE76 Delta E in Lab) 20
    the file has a name and an about required 
    python3 check-skins.py harbour.json

    It prints harbour: ok, or each failure with the colour, the ratio and what it was measured on, and exits 1 on a failure. Fix the failures by moving the named colour's lightness (keep its hue), then run it again.

    Make it good, not only legal

    Passing the checker means it reads. Whether it is pleasant to play on for an evening is design:

    • Calm surfaces, clear lights. Large areas (case, well, caps) muted or pastel; the signal (accent, glow) carries the colour. No large saturated red, green or blue case: a deep red case with a green light looked awful, and so did a pale blue-grey grid on a pale case, where the empty cells washed out.
    • Lit against unlit by lightness, not only hue. About one person in twelve doesn't tell red from green: a lit step has to be clearly lighter (on a dark well) or deeper (on a light well) than an empty one.
    • Not glaring. A light case is off-white, not #ffffff; a dark one is a deep grey or a tinted near-black, not #000000. Avoid pure white text on pure black.
    • One family, eight faces. Each page needs its own identity, because the number keys flip between them, but the set should feel like one product: a theme (a studio rack, a pencil case, a night train), with one material or tone per machine.
    • Mix light and dark cases across the eight, so neighbouring pages change visibly.
    • The header's lights tell the machines apart (the Delta E rule): pick eight glows that differ in hue, then in lightness.
    • A good place to start: a tested scale (Radix, Tailwind, Material, OKLCH ramps), not a guess. For a dark machine: case 950–900, well a step darker, rail a mid step of the same hue, ink 50, accent 400–500, glow a lighter step of the accent. For a light machine: case 50–100, well 100–200 (or a dark inset), ink 950, accent 600–700.

    Put it on a MIKO

    1. From a USB stick: copy the file into ravving/presets/skins/ on the stick. On the MIKO, MENU → the stick's tile opens the USB page; it copies presets both ways, skins among them.
    2. Over the network (developers): copy it to /zynthian/zynthian-my-data/ravving/skins/ on the device, for example with scp harbour.json root@<device>:/zynthian/zynthian-my-data/ravving/skins/. Write the file in one go (copy to another name, then rename), so ravving never reads it half written.
    3. SETTINGS → DEVICE → SKINS: the set is there by its name, with swatches of its colours. Tap it: the pages change at once, and the pick stays after a restart.

    To share a set, share the file.

    Checklist for a new set

    1. Pick a theme and write the name and about.
    2. Choose eight cases (some light, some dark) and eight distinct lights.
    3. Fill all fourteen keys for all eight machines.
    4. Run check-skins.py until it prints ok.
    5. Look at every page on the device (1 to 5, then 5 again for the PAD SAMPLER, and 1 again for DRUMS 2 and 3): an empty grid, a full one, and while playing.
    6. Hand over the file, the checker's output, and a line per machine on why its colours.
