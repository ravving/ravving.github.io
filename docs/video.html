    Using ravving / docs/video.md

    Video recordings and the computer link

    A video of the session with the master's sound, the quality tiers measured, the mic, the keys shown on screen, and the laptop files are sent to over SSH.

    A video of a session: the screen (or a USB camera) with the master's sound, as one MP4 in the library's videos/ folder (Paths::videos(), /zynthian/zynthian-my-data/ravving/videos on the device), or the sound alone as a WAV among the TRACKS ("Audio only" below). The control center's LIVE REC card starts one; while LIVE REC is on, the device's RECORD key starts a recording and STOP ends it, from any page, as the header's ● and ■ do on the screen. SETTINGS · VIDEO has the QUALITY, the TIMER (a countdown before it starts) and the SOURCE; VIDEO SAVED, when a recording ends, sends it to the COMPUTER set up in SETTINGS or copies it TO USB. Code: src/backend/videorecorder.* (video), src/backend/audiotap.*, src/backend/computerlink.* (computer), qml/components/ComputerPopup.qml, VideoSavedPopup.qml, the VIDEO card in ControlCenter.qml.

    The rule it is built on

    The sound comes first: a video may cost the audio nothing it can hear, and the audio in the video must never drop. Everything below follows from that.

    How a recording is made

    1. The sound is tapped, not recorded through AudioLevels. AudioTap is ravving's own JACK client (ravving-video, stereo in) connected to the master's last outputs (what the speakers get: the LIVE FX inserts included, which the header's ● WAV recording misses). Its process callback copies each cycle into a ring of 2^20 frames (21.8 s at 48 kHz) and touches nothing else; a plain thread drains the ring into a named pipe ($XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/ ravving-video-<stamp>.pipe, 1 MB of kernel buffering) as raw 32-bit float stereo. ffmpeg reads the pipe as -f f32le. Why not libzynthbox's AudioLevels (the WAV recorder's and the exporter's path): it runs one recording at a time, and a video session must leave the header's ● and the samplers' record-to-pad free. Why not ffmpeg's own -f jack input: its ring is 16 periods (85 ms at 256 frames), so an encoder or SD-card stall longer than that drops sound; this ring holds 21 seconds, and what it could not hold is counted (audioDroppedMs, a warning in the issues center) rather than lost quietly.
    2. The picture is ffmpeg's. x11grab of the display (-draw_mouse 0), or v4l2 of the camera, encoded with libx264 on two threads at nice -n 10: the Pi 5 has no hardware H.264 encoder (ffmpeg lists h264_v4l2m2m, but the Pi 5's V4L2 nodes are its ISP and HEVC decoder). The tiers, measured below, all run with 0 xruns.
    3. Sync without a shared clock. ffmpeg stamps every grabbed frame with the wall clock (-use_wallclock_as_timestamps 1; on this ffmpeg x11grab's default stamps are the wall clock too). The tap takes the wall-clock moment of its first sample on the audio thread, from the cycle's own start (jack_get_cycle_times: the cycle began now − (jack_get_time() − cycle_usecs) ago), and ffmpeg is told it with -itsoffset on the audio input. With -copyts both streams sit on the same absolute clock in the file, to the millisecond.
    4. Matroska first, then one remux. The recording goes to .recording-<stamp>.mkv: a Matroska is readable even if ravving or ffmpeg dies (an MP4 without its index is not). On STOP the tap closes the pipe (ffmpeg's audio input ends), ffmpeg gets SIGINT and finishes its file, and ffprobe reads the two streams' start times: the audio began while ffmpeg was starting (~0.45 s before the first frame), so ffmpeg -ss <video start − audio start> -i rec.mkv -c copy -movflags +faststart cuts the head to the first video frame, writes the MP4 with its index up front and its title / date tags, and the Matroska is removed. The remux copies packets: a few seconds for a long recording ("SAVING" on the card and the plate). If the remux fails the Matroska is kept under the final name.

    Checked on milo with tools/video-sync-check.sh (the mechanism alone, with a synthetic audio feeder): video − audio in the Matroska 0.466 s (ffmpeg's launch latency), the cut MP4's video at 0.000 s and its audio at 0.003 s (the AAC frame granularity at -c copy).

    Measured on milo (Pi 5, 2026-10-11)

    tools/video-encode-impact.sh: each tier recording the screen while the transport played the open project, tools/audio-impact.sh 20 playing alongside (frames/s is the UI's own rate, which must not fall; "realtime" is ravving's audio threads' CPU; xruns must be 0).

    tier ffmpeg CPU (of one core) UI frames/s realtime threads xruns frames delivered
    none (baseline) — 21.5 18 % 0 —
    full screen 30 fps, ultrafast 35 % 21.1 23 % 0 991 in 33 s (30.0 fps)
    HIGH: full screen 30 fps, veryfast crf 21 48 % 20.7 23 % 0 976 in 32 s (30.0)
    MEDIUM: full screen 15 fps, veryfast crf 23 26 % 21.1 21 % 0 496 in 32 s (15.0)
    LOW: 640 × 400 30 fps, veryfast crf 26 31 % 21.8 23 % 0 1021 in 34 s (30.0)

    So every tier records at its full frame rate with the audio graph untouched; the realtime threads cost 3–5 points more of CPU under the encoder's memory traffic, and the UI keeps its rate. MEDIUM is the default (the lightest; the screen's text stays sharp at full size). LOW makes the smallest files, not the lightest load (the scale filter costs what the pixels save). The AAC audio is 192 kb/s. File sizes: ~1 MB per 30 s at MEDIUM with a still screen; a busy screen (the playhead, meters, the SONG page's waves) is several times that.

    A microphone in the sound

    SETTINGS · VIDEO · MIC: the RECORDER's input, as it is set up there (MIC / GUITAR / LINE, the jack in use, its GAIN, its effects), mixed into the video's sound. Nothing of the input is repeated for the video: InputRecorder::setListener(ports) adds the tap's two mic ports (ravving-video:mic_l / mic_r) as one more destination at the end of the input chain (JackWiring::setInputChain's listeners, beside the monitor lane and the take recorder; a mono input feeds both), the input counts as in use while a listener is set (its effects run), and the audio thread adds the ports to the master's sound as the video hears it. The master itself is untouched: the speakers get the mic only with the RECORDER's MONITOR on, in which case the video hears it twice (louder), so one or the other. tools/video-test.sh records once with MIC on and checks the link; tools/video-mic-check.sh is the listening test: a recording with the music stopped while someone speaks, then the file's audio measured. Measured on milo (2026-10-11, a mic on jack 5, the RECORDER on MIC at 30 dB gain): the chain found jack 5 on its own (capture_FR → mic_l), the file's audio peaked at −1.4 dBFS, RMS −21.6 dB, the music stopped: the voice, at the gain set on the RECORDER page.

    The voice over

    A voice over the playing (a tutorial, a jam with talk) was buried: the master plays some 25 dB above a voice at a normal distance, and a plain sum leaves the voice behind it. With MIC on, the tap mixes the two as a voice over (AudioTap::setVoiceOver, SETTINGS · VIDEO · VOICE):

    • the mic is made crisp: a 120 Hz high-pass and a +4 dB high shelf from 3 kHz (RBJ biquads on the audio thread), then lifted by VOICE LIFT (+6 dB by default, +12 or flat);
    • its envelope is followed (instant attack, 250 ms release); while it is above the voice threshold the master is turned down to VOICE DUCK (−12 dB by default, −6 or −18; 0 is off) in 20 ms and comes back over 600 ms once the voice stops, so the music sits in the background exactly while someone speaks;
    • the threshold follows the music (since 2026-10-12, in place of a fixed −34 dBFS): it is −34 dBFS at least, and 12 dB over what the speakers are expected to leak into the mic right now, i.e. the master's envelope (as ducked) times a leak ratio the tap learns as the mic's level over the master's: down within a second, up over 8 s, never past −26 dB of the master (0.02, −34 dB, to start; from the moment VIDEO is on, since the mic is wired then). A fixed threshold made the beat duck itself through a mic near the speakers and pump as the duck let go and the leak came back over it; against the expected leak, only a voice counts. The learning is slow upwards on purpose: the first cut learned up fast while no voice was heard, and a steady −20 dBFS tone under the threshold became "leak" within a second and never ducked (tools/video-voice-check.sh caught it). What the cap means: a mic that hears the speakers louder than −26 dB of the master (the mic in front of the speaker) still ducks itself, and a leak between the start value and the cap ducks the beat for a second or two at its start, until the ratio has climbed. The RECORDER's GAIN scales the voice and the leak alike, so the ratio holds when it is turned;
    • the sum goes through a look-ahead peak limiter (1 ms ahead, ceiling −1 dBFS, 150 ms release): the mix runs through a 1 ms delay line, the loudest sample in the line sets the gain, which gets down over a quarter of the line, so a hot mic over a loud beat is turned down before the peak comes out, never clipped, and never with the click of a gain step. The first cut (2026-10-12) clamped the sum instead, and a mic peaking near 0 dBFS lifted 6 dB came out as crackle with runs of samples stuck at the ceiling; a limiter without look-ahead replaced it the same day, the look-ahead the day after. The 1 ms sits inside the measured 3 ms of audio offset; the master without the mic is not delayed.

    The RECORDER's GAIN still sets how hot the voice comes in, and the recorder watches it: while VIDEO is on with MIC the input is wired into the tap at once (not only while recording) and its peak is metered ten times a second (micLevel, before the lift). SETTINGS · VIDEO shows it on a LEVEL bar with the −6 dB mark, and the project plate carries it standing in its right corner while VIDEO is on (red while hot, a blue dot at its foot while the music ducks). A peak over −6 dBFS within the last two seconds is micHot: the bar turns red and the settings say MIC TOO HOT, turn the GAIN down; a mic peaking around −12 dBFS leaves the mix room and the limiter idle.

    The voice over is for a voice: with the RECORDER on GUITAR or LINE the input is summed as it is (through the limiter), nothing ducks under a guitar, and the VOICE rows say so. The settings apply at once, on a recording under way too. With DUCK and LIFT both 0 the mix is the plain sum. tools/video-voice-check.sh measures it: a steady 300 Hz tone at −12 dBFS into the tap's master ports as the music (so the measure does not depend on the project open: a sparse beat swings 30 dB inside a second, which hid the duck on 2026-10-12; MUSIC=project plays the project instead), a 1 kHz tone in bursts (tools/tone.py, −20 dBFS, TONE_DB for another level) into the tap's mic ports as the voice; second by second, the music must be DUCK dB quieter while the tone is on, the tone must come out LIFT dB louder, the file must show no clipping (its peak under −0.5 dBFS, no run of samples stuck at the ceiling), and the meter must read the tone (video.micLevel near −20 dBFS while a burst plays, micHot false; a −3 dBFS burst sets micHot).

    The hardware keys and the UI

    • The LIVE REC card (control center): a tap opens the LIVE REC dialog (LiveRecPopup.qml): WHAT, a VIDEO (the screen or a USB camera with the master's sound, an MP4 among the VIDEOS) or AUDIO ONLY (the sound alone, a 24-bit WAV among the TRACKS, see below); MIC, the RECORDER's input in the sound; and START, which switches LIVE REC on and begins after the TIMER (the dialog is the confirmation the card asked for with a second tap until 2026-10-12: a stray tap never starts one; the drawer closes first, so it is not in the picture; the TIMER counts down on an overlay that is not recorded either, since ffmpeg starts after it). ◀ ▶ pick WHAT, ▲ ▼ toggle the MIC, SELECT starts. The card reads LIVE REC · READY / REC IN n / REC mm:ss / LIVE REC · SAVING and is lit while LIVE REC is on; a tap while on ends the recording and switches LIVE REC off. A new project (NEW in the project menu) switches LIVE REC off too, a recording under way saved first (Projects::created).
    • While VIDEO is on, RECORD and STOP are the video's before any page sees them (Ravving::handleCuia → VideoRecorder::takesKey): RECORD starts when idle (a toast says ■ stops when it is already recording), STOP ends the recording (and stops the music with it: the session is over) or cancels the countdown; STOP with nothing recording is the transport's again. The header's ● and ■ do the same (Session.toggleRecording / Session.stop).
    • The project plate shows the video's state while VIDEO is on (VIDEO READY · ● RECORDS, VIDEO IN 3, ● REC 01:23, VIDEO · SAVING); the RECORD LED glows dim red when ready, orange during the countdown, red while recording; STOP lights while there is something to end.
    • While a video records, every device key pressed (ALT combos as "ALT + MODE", knob turns as "KNOB 1 ▲", "BIG KNOB ▼") is named in a short red notice at the screen's bottom (Ravving::keyShown → Main.qml's second Toast), so the video shows what was played; SETTINGS · VIDEO · KEYS turns it off (video.showKeys, on by default). An audio recording shows none: there is no picture to explain.
    • VIDEO SAVED / LIVE REC SAVED (when a recording ends): the file, its length, size and picture size; DELETE at the left (a ConfirmButton: the second tap within three seconds removes the file, VideoRecorder::remove), RENAME and SEND… at the right (SendPopup: a USB stick's ravving/videos or ravving/exports, a computer, the phone); an audio recording also has METADATA… (below).
    • A failure (ffmpeg missing, no space, the encoder giving no frames in 12 s, ffmpeg dying) is a toast, an issues-center entry and video.error; what ffmpeg wrote before dying is still saved when it has frames.

    Audio only

    LIVE REC's AUDIO ONLY (video.mode "audio", VideoRecorder::audioOnly) is the same recorder without the picture: the tap feeds ffmpeg as before, but the command has no video input and no encoder, only -c:a pcm_s24le -f wav into .recording-<name>.wav in the exports folder (Paths::exports(), the TRACKS the music player and FILES list), so nothing is encoded and nothing has to be lined up (no -copyts, no -itsoffset). The first bytes out (ffmpeg's out_time_us in the progress report) stand for the first frame; stopping renames the WAV to <project> - <date>.wav (no remux, nothing to cut: the sound begins where it began). The MIC, the voice over and the limiter apply as to a video. The key notices are not shown.

    When it ends, LIVE REC SAVED offers, besides DELETE, RENAME and SEND…, METADATA…: the export dialog over the file (ExportPopup.openForFile, the same form without WHAT and the lengths; SAVE WITH TAGS), which calls Exporter::finishRecording(rawFile, metadata): the WAV is written whole in the FORMAT chosen (WAV 24-bit, FLAC, OGG, MP3) under the NAME given in the exports folder, tagged with the TITLE, ARTIST, ALBUM, GENRE, COMPOSER and COMMENT, its cover drawn and saved beside it, its sidecar JSON written, the raw WAV removed, and the files copied to the stick or sent to the computer picked. That is the track export's own finishing (Exporter::writeAndTag, shared since 2026-10-12: one path for both), so a live recording and an exported track are the same kind of file afterwards. The tagged track then stands for the recording in LIVE REC SAVED (VideoRecorder::adopt), so RENAME and SEND… act on it. Without METADATA… the plain WAV stays, playable and listed as any track.

    tools/video-test.sh has the audio checks: a recording in AUDIO ONLY mode lands as a WAV among the TRACKS, ffprobe sees one audio stream of the right length, finishRecording over the fifo writes the FLAC with its cover and sidecar and takes the WAV away.

    The computer link

    SETTINGS · COMPUTERS holds the laptops or PCs exports and videos can be sent to over SSH: any number (Computers, computers, a list of ComputerLinks, each its own group computers/<id> of the settings file; a set-up from before the list is moved into c1). ADD A COMPUTER asks for the host name or address, the user there, and that user's password; a computer already in the list as the same user@host is set up again rather than added twice. The password is used once: it opens the first connection so the device's own key (~/.config/ravving/ssh/id_ed25519, made on the first CONNECT) can be installed with ssh-copy-id; it is never written to the settings. The password reaches ssh through SSH_ASKPASS (a helper script of ravving's printing a file readable by root alone, made for the set-up's seconds and removed after, SSH_ASKPASS_REQUIRE=force so no terminal is involved). The computer's host key is kept in ravving's own known_hosts (accepted on the first connection, refused if it changes; FORGET removes that computer's entry with ssh-keygen -R, the others stay). The Downloads folder is asked of the computer (xdg-user-dir DOWNLOAD, else ~/Downloads); files go there with scp, one at a time (busy / phase / progress), and sent(ok, count, label) ends it with a toast. Statuses: unset, setting-up, ready, unreachable, refused (the key no longer accepted: CONNECT AGAIN with the password), failed (error says why: a wrong password, a name that does not resolve, SSH refused or no answer, a changed host key). Where it is used: one standard SEND… button (SendPopup, the destinations there are: every writable USB stick's ravving/ folder through RemovableDrives::copyFiles, each computer, and a phone by QR code: see "Sending to a phone" in README.md) in the FILES browser (every kind: a project is bundled for the destination by Exporter::exportProjectFile, a preset written out as one JSON file by FileBrowser::presetFile), on VIDEO SAVED, on each track in the music player, in the pattern presets dialog and the sound picker; and the export dialog's COMPUTER row (a chip per computer, one lit), which sends a new track or bundle as soon as it is written (Exporter::sendToComputer, the metadata's computer = the computer's id).

    The computer needs an SSH server: Linux openssh-server (Arch: systemctl enable --now sshd), macOS "Remote Login"; Windows' OpenSSH server has no $HOME/Downloads in its default shell and is not supported.

    Traps and limits

    • No probing on the pipe input (-probesize 32 -analyzeduration 0 before its -i): with ffmpeg's defaults, avformat_find_stream_info reads 5 s of the raw audio before the output is opened, so the first progress report (and ravving's "recording" state) came 6 s after the launch, while 75 raw frames (4 MB each) piled up in the video input's queue meanwhile. Measured: the first frame= report at 5.1 s with the defaults, 1.8 s without probing; the picture itself starts 0.5 s after the launch either way (the x264 lookahead and the stats period account for the rest). The video input's queue is 64 frames for the same reason.
    • -t and -copyts do not mix: ffmpeg's -t compares against the absolute timestamps, so a recording limited with -t ends at once. Recordings are ended with SIGINT (once; a second signal makes ffmpeg quit without its trailer: -nostdin, and never timeout -s INT in a non-interactive shell, which sent two).
    • The wall clock: a step of the system clock during a recording (the Pi has no RTC; timesyncd steps it on the first network sync after boot) puts a jump in the video's timestamps (a freeze for a forward step; dropped frames for a backward one). Start recording once the device has been online for a moment.
    • A test that pkill -fs a script by name over ssh kills the ssh command line itself when it carries that name (every command after it is lost without a word): pkill -x ffmpeg, and patterns that cannot match the caller.
    • ffprobe on a Matroska written with -copyts reports the absolute starts (epoch seconds); avg_frame_rate is right, nb_frames is N/A (count with -count_frames).
    • The AAC encoder's priming frame puts the audio stream's start 21 ms before the first sample; the remux reads the stream starts from the file, so the cut is consistent with it.
    • The screen recording shows exactly what is on the display: a dialog opened during the recording is in the picture; the countdown overlay and the drawer are not (both are gone before ffmpeg starts).
    • Camera: USB cameras are found in /sys/class/video4linux (the node with index 0 on a USB device); the capture size follows the tier (640 × 480 for LOW, 1280 × 720 otherwise). Not tested yet: no camera was at hand (2026-10-11).
    • Streaming later: the same tap, encoder, tiers, keys and UI would feed -f flv rtmp://… (YouTube, Twitch, an OBS on the computer) instead of the Matroska; what is new there is the network sink and a bitrate budget for Wi-Fi, not the capture.
