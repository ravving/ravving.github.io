    The designs / docs/streaming-plan.md

    Streaming

    Where a live stream would go from the video recorder's tap.

    Research of 2026-10-11, after the video recorder was built (docs/video.md). The question: could ravving stream a live session to Twitch or YouTube from the Zynthbox itself, while the user plays, and how feasible is it? Short answer: yes, feasible with what the device has, because the hard part (capturing the screen and the master's sound without dropping audio, encoding at a cost the audio graph does not feel) is done and measured; a stream adds a network sink, a stream key, a bitrate budget and a reconnect strategy. The rest of this file says how, what each platform wants, what was measured, the design, the risks and the phases.

    What the platforms want

    Twitch (developer guide, ingest list; the Broadcasting Guidelines page itself needs JavaScript and did not load, so its numbers come from Restream, StreamYard and BlazingCDN: third-party, consistent with each other; confirm on Twitch's page before shipping): - Ingest: rtmp://<ingest-server>/app/<stream-key>; the generic entry point is rtmp://live.twitch.tv/app/<key>; GET https://ingest.twitch.tv/ingests lists the nearest servers (url_template). ?bandwidthtest=true on the URL streams without going live, for a bandwidth check in Twitch Inspector: the way to test from the device without an audience. - The stream key comes from the creator dashboard (Settings → Stream); it is a secret that anyone can stream to the channel with. - Video: H.264, CBR, a key frame every 2 s, 720p30 at about 3000 kbps (4500 for 720p60 / 1080p30), 6000 kbps the practical ceiling. Audio: AAC-LC 128–160 kbps, 44.1 or 48 kHz. - RTMPS: Twitch's pages reached did not say; ffmpeg on the device has rtmps anyway.

    YouTube (Choose live encoder settings, official): - Ingest: RTMP / RTMPS (RTMPS recommended; HLS exists as an alternative). The URL and key come from YouTube Studio → Go live → Stream (rtmp://a.rtmp.youtube.com/live2/<key>, rtmps://a.rtmps.youtube.com:443/live2/<key>). A channel must be verified and has a 24-hour wait before its first live stream. - Video: H.264 (or HEVC / AV1), CBR, key frame every 2 s ("do not exceed 4"), CABAC, up to 2 B-frames, square pixels, Rec. 709. H.264 bitrate: 720p30 minimum 3 Mbps, recommended 8; 480p30 minimum 0.4, recommended 4. Audio: AAC, 128 kbps stereo, 44.1 kHz (48 kHz for 5.1).

    Both accept the H.264 + AAC in FLV that ffmpeg's -f flv writes; both want a fixed key frame interval and CBR, which the recorder's -g 2×fps already does and -b:v -maxrate -bufsize adds. Neither needs 16:9: a 1280 × 800 picture is letterboxed by the player; a scale/pad to 1280 × 720 is a cosmetic choice.

    What the device has (measured)

    • ffmpeg 7.1 with the protocols rtmp rtmps rtmpt rtmpts srt rist tls https and the muxers flv, mpegts, hls, tee (one encode, several outputs: a file and the stream at once).
    • The encoder budget (docs/video.md, the transport playing, 0 xruns): full screen at 30 fps 48 % of a core, at 15 fps 26 %, 640 × 400 at 30 fps 31 %. A stream at 720p30 x264 veryfast CBR 3000 kbps sits in the same range (CBR costs no more than CRF); the audio graph did not feel any of them.
    • The sound path: AudioTap's 21-second ring and the 64-frame video queue absorb a network stall of seconds without losing a sample (the recorder's design, reused as is).
    • Wi-Fi (milo on the 5 GHz band, −29 dBm, link 390 Mbit/s): an upload from milo to the laptop over the LAN measured 205 Mbit/s (80 MB over ssh, twice), round trip to the router under 1 ms. The LAN is no limit; the ISP's uplink is the one that matters and was not measured (a home uplink of 10 Mbit/s gives 3× headroom over 3000 kbps; ?bandwidthtest=true or a stream to OBS on the laptop measures the real thing).

    How it would work

    Two targets, one pipeline:

    1. Straight to the platform (Twitch / YouTube): ffmpeg's -f flv rtmp(s)://…/<key> output in place of (or beside, with tee) the recorder's Matroska. Everything else is the recorder's: the tap, -copyts with the wall clock on both inputs, x264 on two threads at nice 10, the countdown, the plate's status, RECORD / STOP by the keys (RECORD starts the stream, STOP ends it; or STREAM is a card of its own and RECORD still records locally). Encoder changes for a stream: -preset veryfast -tune zerolatency (no lookahead: a second less latency, a little less quality), -b:v 3000k -maxrate 3000k -bufsize 6000k (CBR), -g 60 -keyint_min 60 at 30 fps, -profile:v main, -c:a aac -b:a 160k -ar 48000, -flvflags no_duration_filesize, and the output stamped from zero (no -copyts on an FLV: its timestamps are 32-bit milliseconds; the sync holds because both inputs are stamped by the same clock and ffmpeg keeps their offset).
    2. To the computer first (the user's laptop running OBS): ravving sends SRT (-f mpegts srt://laptop:9000?mode=caller) or RTMP to OBS (a Media Source listening on SRT, or an RTMP server such as nginx-rtmp); OBS adds a camera, overlays, chat and the platform account, and streams. This is the robust set-up for a show: the device does one cheap thing (720p over the LAN at 200 Mbit/s), the laptop does the internet and the production, and a stream key never touches the device. It also reuses the COMPUTER link's host name.

    The stream status comes from ffmpeg's -progress (bitrate=, speed=, drop_frames=, out_time): speed under 1.0× for seconds means the uplink is too slow for the bitrate; drop_frames says ffmpeg is dropping to keep up. Shown on the card and the plate as LIVE · 00:12:34 · 3.0 Mb/s, with a warning when it falls behind. A disconnect: ffmpeg exits; ravving restarts it with the same key after 2, 5, 10 s (Twitch keeps a stream alive for about 90 s of silence before ending it; YouTube similar), the tap running on, so the sound is not lost and the stream resumes where the network came back.

    The stream key: typed once (SETTINGS · STREAMING: PLATFORM Twitch / YouTube / Custom URL, the KEY, the QUALITY) and kept in ~/.config/ravving/streaming.conf (0600, root-only on the device, as the SSH key is); shown masked; a 40-character key on the on-screen keyboard is tedious, so also accepted from a ravving/streaming.txt on a USB stick or sent from the computer (the link works both ways over ssh). Never logged (the ffmpeg command line in the journal has it masked).

    Feasibility, by part

    Part Verdict Why
    Capture + sound + encode done the recorder: 0 xruns at every tier, exact sync, a 21 s audio ring
    Network sink (RTMP/RTMPS/SRT) easy ffmpeg has the protocols; a flag change on the command
    CPU for 720p30 CBR fine ≤ 48 % of one core measured at full screen 30 fps; the Pi 5 has four
    Uplink depends on the ISP 3 Mbit/s steady needed; the LAN gives 200; measure with Twitch's bandwidth test
    Latency / drop handling moderate -progress readout + restart with backoff; the ring hides stalls up to 21 s
    Stream key entry and storage moderate typing is painful; USB file / computer link / a webconf page help; 0600 file
    A camera in the picture later a USB camera works as a source already (untested); picture-in-picture (screen + camera) is one ffmpeg overlay filter, more CPU
    Chat / viewers on the device out of scope needs OAuth to the platform APIs; the laptop (OBS) is the place for it
    Viewer-side latency normal 5–15 s on the platforms; the device cannot change that

    Overall: feasible in two or three days of work, most of it UI (the STREAM card, the settings, the status) and testing against a real account; the audio-first rule is already satisfied by the recorder's design. The OBS-on-the-laptop route is the one to do first: it needs no account on the device, no key, is easy to test on the LAN (OBS shows the picture), and is what a real show would use; the direct route is the same code with another URL.

    Plan

    1. Backend Streamer (stream), sharing the recorder's pieces: extract the ffmpeg argument builder and the tap handling of VideoRecorder into a base (CaptureSession), so recording and streaming are two sinks of one capture; a stream can also record locally with -f tee. Settings: platform, url, key, quality (720p30 3000k / 480p30 1500k / audio-only 160k, for a music stream on a weak uplink), reconnects. Status from -progress; the restart loop; Issues entries for a failed connection and a slow uplink.
    2. UI: a STREAM card in the control center's top grid (LIVE · 00:12:34 · 3.0 Mb/s), SETTINGS · STREAMING (platform chips, URL/KEY fields one per row, QUALITY, a TEST button = Twitch's ?bandwidthtest=true for 20 s with the result), the plate's LIVE state, RECORD / STOP as the recorder's (the STREAM card armed: RECORD starts the stream, STOP ends it), a toast on reconnects.
    3. Tests: tools/stream-test.sh against an RTMP listener on the laptop (ffmpeg -listen 1 -i rtmp://0.0.0.0:1935/live/test -c copy out.flv, or OBS): the stream arrives, its bitrate and key frame interval (ffprobe), the sync, audio-impact.sh while streaming (0 xruns), a forced disconnect (the listener killed) and the reconnect, the ring's dropped frames (0).
    4. A real stream to the user's Twitch or YouTube account, with the bandwidth test first, then a short unlisted stream; measure speed and drop_frames over 10 minutes.
    5. Later: a camera picture-in-picture, the sections' names as a title overlay (ffmpeg drawtext, cheap), viewer count through the platform APIs if ever wanted.
