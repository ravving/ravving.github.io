    Live recordings

    Video and audio recording, the microphone and voice over, the computer link.

    How LIVE REC records the screen or the sound: the JACK tap, FFmpeg, the microphone and voice over, the keys shown on a video, and the computer link. The classes are in the API reference.

    LIVE REC records the session. It makes either:

    • a video: the screen or a USB camera, with the master's sound, as one MP4 in the library's videos/ folder (Paths::videos());
    • the sound alone: a WAV among the TRACKS ("Audio only", below).

    The code: src/backend/videorecorder.* (video), src/backend/audiotap.*, src/backend/computerlink.* (computer), qml/components/ComputerPopup.qml, LiveRecPopup.qml, VideoSavedPopup.qml, and the LIVE REC card in ControlCenter.qml.

    The rule it's built on: the sound comes first. A video may cost the audio nothing it can hear, and the audio in the video must never drop.

    How a recording is made

    1. The sound is tapped, not recorded through AudioLevels

    AudioTap is ravving's own JACK client (ravving-video, stereo in), connected to the master's last outputs. That's what the speakers get, LIVE FX inserts included, which the header's ● WAV recording misses.

    • Its process callback copies each cycle into a ring of 2^20 frames (21.8 s at 48 kHz), and touches nothing else.
    • A plain thread drains the ring into a named pipe ($XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/ravving-video-<stamp>.pipe, with 1 MB of kernel buffering), as raw 32-bit float stereo. ffmpeg reads the pipe as -f f32le.

    Why not the other paths:

    • libzynthbox's AudioLevels (the WAV recorder's and the exporter's path) runs one recording at a time. A video session must leave the header's ● and the samplers' record-to-pad free.
    • ffmpeg's own -f jack input has a ring of 16 periods: 85 ms at 256 frames. An encoder or SD-card stall longer than that drops sound. This ring holds 21 seconds, and anything it can't hold is counted (audioDroppedMs, a warning in the issues center), not lost quietly.

    2. The picture is ffmpeg's

    x11grab of the display (-draw_mouse 0), or v4l2 of the camera, encoded with libx264 on two threads at nice -n 10. The Pi 5 has no hardware H.264 encoder: ffmpeg lists h264_v4l2m2m, but the Pi 5's V4L2 nodes are its ISP and HEVC decoder. The tiers ("Limits", below) all run with 0 xruns.

    3. Sync without a shared clock

    • ffmpeg stamps every grabbed frame with the wall clock (-use_wallclock_as_timestamps 1; on this ffmpeg, x11grab's default stamps are the wall clock too).
    • The tap takes the wall-clock moment of its first sample on the audio thread, from the cycle's own start. jack_get_cycle_times says the cycle began now − (jack_get_time() − cycle_usecs) ago.
    • ffmpeg is told that moment with -itsoffset on the audio input. With -copyts, both streams sit on the same absolute clock in the file, to the millisecond.

    4. Matroska first, then one remux

    The recording goes to .recording-<stamp>.mkv. A Matroska is readable even if ravving or ffmpeg dies; an MP4 without its index isn't. On STOP:

    1. The tap closes the pipe, so ffmpeg's audio input ends.
    2. ffmpeg gets SIGINT, and finishes its file.
    3. ffprobe reads the two streams' start times. The audio began while ffmpeg was starting, about 0.45 s before the first frame.
    4. ffmpeg -ss <video start − audio start> -i rec.mkv -c copy -movflags +faststart cuts the head to the first video frame. It writes the MP4 with its index up front and its title and date tags, and the Matroska is removed.

    The remux copies packets, so it takes a few seconds for a long recording ("SAVING" on the card and the plate). If the remux fails, the Matroska is kept under the final name.

    A microphone in the sound

    MIC (the LIVE REC dialog, SETTINGS · LIVE REC) mixes the device input into the recording's sound through the live recording's own input path, video.input (a LiveRecMic). The path has its own INPUT (MIC, GUITAR or LINE), its own GAIN, its role (VOICE-OVER or SINGING) and its own EFFECTS.

    The RECORDER page's input (studio.input, an InputRecorder) is a separate path with its own settings. Nothing set on the RECORDER page reaches a live recording, and nothing set for a live recording reaches the RECORDER.

    One owner of the line

    The input line (the card's jacks and its gain) has one owner at a time.

    • While LIVE REC is on with MIC, the live recording holds the line (InputRecorder::hold("liverec")): studio.input.held is true and heldBy says liverec.
    • The RECORDER wires nothing of its own meanwhile: no take, no monitor, no effects, no tuner. It leaves the card's gain to the holder, and refuses a take with a reason.
    • Every user says why it waits. The RECORDER's plate reads IN THE LIVE RECORDING · ■ TWICE LEAVES LIVE REC; its ● REC, tabs, MONITOR and GAIN wait. The samplers' ● REC reads IN LIVE REC.
    • A RECORDER take already running keeps the line. The live recording then records without the mic (video.input.status is busy, a notice says so) and takes the line the moment the take ends (MIC IN).
    • Released when LIVE REC goes off (STOP twice, the card, a new project), when MIC is switched off, and when the recorder is destroyed (the application leaving). The card gets the RECORDER's gain and bias back, and its paths are wired as its settings want.
    • Never kept across a start. The hold lives in memory only, so a crash or a restart starts free.

    The path

    • Its own effects. video.input.effects is a second InputEffects on the same data/input-fx.json: a preset's uses says who it's offered to (liverec for VOICE-OVER and SINGING; the rest to both). Its jalv clients are ravving-in-liverec-<preset>-<n>, the RECORDER's ravving-in-recorder-<preset>-<n>; only the preset in use runs, and only while the line is held.
    • Its own wiring. The jack in use goes through the live chain into the tap's two mic ports (ravving-video:mic_l / mic_r): JackWiring::setInputChain("liverec", …), a chain of its own beside the RECORDER's "recorder". The jack is the RECORDER's activeJack, which follows the signal between takes and serves both. A mono input feeds both ports.
    • The card is one. The holder sets its PGA gain and mic bias (InputCard::apply); the live recording keeps a gain per mode of its own.
    • No monitor. The live mic never reaches the speakers (a mic by the speakers howls); the tap hears it, the room hears the person.
    • The audio thread adds the mic ports to the master's sound, as the recording hears it. The master itself is untouched.

    VOICE-OVER and SINGING

    The role (video.input.role, the VOICE chips) says how the voice sits in the recording, and brings its own chain:

    • VOICE-OVER: the music ducks under the voice (the DUCK setting). The chain is dry and close: a 100 Hz high-pass, a gate on the pauses, 4:1 compression, a de-esser and a presence lift (GATE, SQUASH and PRESENCE knobs).
    • SINGING: the music stays as it is under the voice, and the chain has room: an 80 Hz high-pass, gentle compression, a little air, a tempo echo and a plate (EVEN, ECHO and SPACE knobs).

    Any effect on the voice

    Picking a role loads its chain, and the first run loads it by itself. EFFECTS may then change it to any other (TAME, ROBOT, OCTAVE…), and the role keeps saying whether the music ducks (video.input.ducks). The role picked again brings its chain back.

    On GUITAR or LINE there is no role: the input is summed as it is, and a role picked there waits for the mic.

    Mode, gain, role, preset and knobs are the live recording's own settings, kept between sessions (ravving/video, ravving/liverec-fx).

    tools/liverec-mic-test.sh checks all of it on the device (testing.md). tools/video-test.sh records once with MIC on and checks the link. tools/video-mic-check.sh is the listening test: a recording with the music stopped while someone speaks, then the file's audio measured.

    The voice over

    With MIC on in the VOICE-OVER role, the tap mixes the master and the mic as a voice over (AudioTap::setVoiceOver, SETTINGS · LIVE REC · DUCK and LIFT). A plain sum leaves a voice at a normal distance some 25 dB under the master. In the SINGING role the duck is off and the lift stays.

    The voice

    The mic is made crisp: a 120 Hz high-pass, and a +4 dB high shelf from 3 kHz (RBJ biquads on the audio thread). Then VOICE LIFT lifts it: +6 dB by default, +12, or flat.

    The duck

    The mic's envelope is followed, with an instant attack and a 250 ms release. While it's above the voice threshold, the master turns down to VOICE DUCK: −12 dB by default, −6 or −18, and 0 is off.

    It ducks in 20 ms, and comes back over 600 ms once the voice stops. So the music sits in the background exactly while someone speaks.

    With DUCK and LIFT both 0, the mix is the plain sum, still through the limiter.

    The threshold follows the music

    The threshold is at least −34 dBFS, and 12 dB over what the speakers are expected to leak into the mic right now. That expected leak is the master's envelope, as ducked, times a leak ratio the tap learns as the mic's level over the master's.

    • The ratio moves down within a second and up over 8 s, never past −26 dB of the master. It starts at 0.02 (−34 dB), from the moment VIDEO is on, since the mic is wired then.
    • Against the expected leak, only a voice counts, so the beat doesn't duck itself through a mic near the speakers. The learning is slow upwards, so a steady tone under the threshold doesn't become "leak".
    • The limits: a mic that hears the speakers louder than −26 dB of the master (in front of the speaker) still ducks itself. A leak between the start value and the cap ducks the beat for a second or two at its start, until the ratio has climbed.
    • The live mic's GAIN scales the voice and the leak alike, so the ratio holds when it's turned.

    The limiter

    The sum goes through a look-ahead peak limiter: 1 ms ahead, a ceiling of −1 dBFS, and a 150 ms release.

    • The mix runs through a 1 ms delay line. The loudest sample in the line sets the gain, which comes down over a quarter of the line.
    • So a hot mic over a loud beat is turned down before the peak comes out: never clipped, and never with the click of a gain step.
    • The 1 ms sits inside the 3 ms of audio offset. The master without the mic isn't delayed.

    The mic's level

    The live mic's GAIN (SETTINGS · LIVE REC) sets how hot the voice comes in, and the recorder watches it.

    • While LIVE REC is on with MIC and the line is held, the input is wired into the tap at once, not only while recording. Its peak is metered ten times a second (micLevel, before the lift).
    • SETTINGS · LIVE REC shows it on a LEVEL bar with a −6 dB mark.
    • The project plate carries it standing in its right corner while VIDEO is on: red while hot, with a blue dot at its foot while the music ducks.
    • micHot: a peak over −6 dBFS within the last two seconds. The bar turns red, and the settings say MIC TOO HOT: turn the GAIN down. A mic peaking around −12 dBFS leaves the mix room and the limiter idle.

    Guitar and line

    The voice over is for a voice. With the live mic's INPUT on GUITAR or LINE, the input is summed as it is, through the limiter, and nothing ducks under a guitar; the rows say so. The settings apply at once, on a recording under way too.

    Checking it

    tools/video-voice-check.sh measures the voice over:

    • The music: a steady 300 Hz tone at −12 dBFS into the tap's master ports, so the measure doesn't depend on the project open. MUSIC=project plays the project instead.
    • The voice: a 1 kHz tone in bursts (tools/tone.py, −20 dBFS; TONE_DB for another level) into the tap's mic ports.
    • Second by second, the music must be DUCK dB quieter while the tone is on, and the tone must come out LIFT dB louder.
    • No clipping: the file's peak under −0.5 dBFS, and no run of samples stuck at the ceiling.
    • The meter must read the tone: video.micLevel near −20 dBFS while a burst plays, with micHot false. A −3 dBFS burst sets micHot.

    The hardware keys and the UI

    The LIVE REC dialog

    A tap on the control center's LIVE REC card opens the LIVE REC dialog (LiveRecPopup.qml):

    • WHAT: a VIDEO (the screen or a USB camera with the master's sound, an MP4 among the VIDEOS), or AUDIO ONLY (the sound alone, a 24-bit WAV among the TRACKS, below);
    • MIC: the recording's own input in the sound, and its role, VOICE-OVER or SINGING;
    • START: switches LIVE REC on, and begins after the TIMER.

    The dialog is the confirmation, so a stray tap never starts a recording. The drawer closes first, so it isn't in the picture, and the TIMER counts down on an overlay that isn't recorded either, since ffmpeg starts after it. ◀ ▶ pick WHAT, ▲ ▼ toggle the MIC, and SELECT starts.

    The card

    It reads LIVE REC · READY, REC IN n, REC mm:ss or LIVE REC · SAVING, and is lit while LIVE REC is on. A tap while it's on ends the recording and switches LIVE REC off. A new project (NEW in the project menu) switches LIVE REC off too, saving a recording under way first (Projects::created).

    RECORD and STOP

    While VIDEO is on, RECORD and STOP are the video's, before any page sees them (Ravving::handleCuia → VideoRecorder::takesKey):

    • RECORD starts when idle. When already recording, a toast says ■ stops it.
    • STOP ends the recording, and stops the music with it: the session is over. During the countdown, it cancels. A toast says ■ AGAIN LEAVES LIVE REC.
    • STOP again within three seconds leaves LIVE REC (VideoRecorder::stopPressed, SecondPress): armed off, the mic's line given back, LIVE REC OFF. Three seconds is the window every second press in ravving uses (AGAIN?, PRESS AGAIN, DELETE).
    • STOP with nothing recording is the transport's (not consumed) and arms the same window, so STOP STOP leaves LIVE REC from the ready state too.
    • The header's ● and ■ do the same (Session.toggleRecording / Session.stop, which calls stopPressed).

    What shows while recording

    • The project plate shows the video's state while VIDEO is on: VIDEO READY · ● RECORDS, VIDEO IN 3, ● REC 01:23, VIDEO · SAVING.
    • The RECORD LED glows dim red when ready, orange during the countdown, and red while recording. STOP lights while there's something to end.
    • The keys pressed: while a video records, every device key pressed is named in a short red notice at the screen's bottom (Ravving::keyShown → Main.qml's second Toast), so the video shows what was played. ALT combos read "ALT + MODE", and knob turns "KNOB 1 ▲" or "BIG KNOB ▼".
    • Turning it off: SETTINGS · VIDEO · KEYS (video.showKeys, on by default). An audio recording shows none: there's no picture to explain.

    When it ends

    VIDEO SAVED or LIVE REC SAVED shows the file, its length, size and picture size.

    • DELETE, at the left, is a ConfirmButton: a second tap within three seconds removes the file (VideoRecorder::remove).
    • RENAME and SEND…, at the right. SEND… opens the SendPopup: a USB stick's ravving/videos or ravving/exports, a computer, or the phone.
    • METADATA…, for an audio recording (below).

    A failure is a toast, an issues-center entry and video.error: ffmpeg missing, no space, the encoder giving no frames in 12 s, or ffmpeg dying. What ffmpeg wrote before dying is still saved when it has frames.

    Audio only

    LIVE REC's AUDIO ONLY (video.mode "audio", VideoRecorder::audioOnly) is the same recorder without the picture.

    • The command has no video input and no encoder. The tap feeds ffmpeg as for a video, and ffmpeg writes -c:a pcm_s24le -f wav into .recording-<name>.wav in the exports folder (Paths::exports(), the TRACKS the music player and FILES list).
    • Nothing is encoded, and nothing has to be lined up: no -copyts, no -itsoffset. The first bytes out (ffmpeg's out_time_us in the progress report) stand for the first frame.
    • Stopping renames the WAV to <project> - <date>.wav. There's no remux and nothing to cut: the sound begins where it began.
    • The MIC, the voice over and the limiter apply as for a video. The key notices aren't shown.

    METADATA…

    When it ends, LIVE REC SAVED offers METADATA… beside DELETE, RENAME and SEND…. It's the export dialog over the file (ExportPopup.openForFile), the same form without WHAT and the lengths, with SAVE WITH TAGS. It calls Exporter::finishRecording(rawFile, metadata):

    1. The WAV is written whole in the FORMAT chosen (WAV 24-bit, FLAC, OGG, MP3), under the NAME given, in the exports folder.
    2. It's tagged with the TITLE, ARTIST, ALBUM, GENRE, COMPOSER and COMMENT. Its cover is drawn and saved beside it, and its sidecar JSON written.
    3. The raw WAV is removed, and the files are copied to the stick or sent to the computer picked.

    That's the track export's own finishing (Exporter::writeAndTag, one path for both), so a live recording and an exported track are the same kind of file afterwards. The tagged track then stands for the recording in LIVE REC SAVED (VideoRecorder::adopt), so RENAME and SEND… act on it. Without METADATA…, the plain WAV stays, playable and listed like any track.

    The computer link

    SETTINGS · COMPUTERS holds the laptops or PCs that exports and videos can be sent to, over SSH. There can be any number: Computers (computers) is a list of ComputerLinks, each its own group computers/<id> of the settings file. An older version's single-computer set-up moves into c1.

    Adding a computer

    ADD A COMPUTER asks for the host name or address, the user there, and that user's password. A computer already in the list as the same user@host is set up again rather than added twice.

    • The password is used once. It opens the first connection, so the device's own key (~/.config/ravving/ssh/id_ed25519, made on the first CONNECT) can be installed with ssh-copy-id. It's never written to the settings.
    • It reaches ssh through SSH_ASKPASS: a helper script of ravving's that prints a file readable by root alone, made for the set-up's seconds and removed after. SSH_ASKPASS_REQUIRE=force means no terminal is involved.
    • The computer's host key is kept in ravving's own known_hosts: accepted on the first connection, and refused if it changes. FORGET removes that computer's entry with ssh-keygen -R; the others stay.

    Sending

    • The destination is the computer's Downloads folder: xdg-user-dir DOWNLOAD, else ~/Downloads.
    • Files go there with scp, one at a time (busy / phase / progress), and sent(ok, count, label) ends it with a toast.
    • The statuses: unset, setting-up, ready, unreachable, refused (the computer refused the key: CONNECT AGAIN with the password), and failed. error says why it failed: a wrong password, a name that doesn't resolve, SSH refused or no answer, or a changed host key.

    Where it's used

    One standard SEND… button (SendPopup) offers every writable USB stick's ravving/ folder (through RemovableDrives::copyFiles), each computer, and a phone by QR code ("Sending to a phone" in the manual). It's in:

    • the FILES browser, for every kind: a project is bundled for the destination by Exporter::exportProjectFile, and a preset written out as one JSON file by FileBrowser::presetFile;
    • VIDEO SAVED, each track in the music player, the pattern presets dialog and the sound picker.

    The export dialog also has a COMPUTER row, a chip per computer with one lit. It sends a new track or bundle as soon as it's written (Exporter::sendToComputer, with the metadata's computer set to the computer's id).

    The computer's side

    It needs an SSH server: Linux openssh-server (Arch: systemctl enable --now sshd), or macOS "Remote Login". Windows' OpenSSH server has no $HOME/Downloads in its default shell, and isn't supported.

    Limits

    • Stopping: recordings end with SIGINT, once; a second signal makes ffmpeg quit without its trailer. -t is never used, since it compares against the absolute timestamps -copyts keeps.
    • Probing: the pipe input isn't probed (-probesize 32 -analyzeduration 0), or ffmpeg would read 5 s of raw audio before opening the output. The video input's queue is 64 frames.
    • The system clock: a step during a recording puts a jump in the video's timestamps. The Pi has no RTC, and timesyncd steps the clock on the first network sync after boot, so start recording once the device has been online for a moment.
    • What's in the picture: exactly what's on the display. A dialog opened during the recording is in it; the countdown overlay and the drawer aren't.
    • USB cameras are found in /sys/class/video4linux: the node with index 0 on a USB device. The capture size follows the tier: 640 × 480 for LOW, 1280 × 720 otherwise.

    The quality tiers

    SETTINGS · VIDEO · QUALITY, with AAC audio at 192 kb/s:

    Tier Picture Encoder
    HIGH full screen, 30 fps veryfast, crf 21
    MEDIUM (the default) full screen, 15 fps veryfast, crf 23
    LOW 640 × 400, 30 fps veryfast, crf 26

    All three run with 0 xruns on a Pi 5. tools/video-encode-impact.sh measures them; mirror any change to the encoder arguments there.
