    The genre packs

    The pattern preset packs for a USB stick: what each preset is, its tempo, its sound, the scale it is written in and the template it ships with.

    Twenty-two packs of pattern presets, ready to load from a USB stick. They don't come installed with ravving. Each one is written in a style, often after the bands and records that define it.

    Group Packs
    Genres Classical, Hip-Hop, Pop, Rock, Trap, Reggaeton, Punk, Tropical, Cumbia, Folk, Heavy Metal, Black Metal, Post-Punk
    Moods and eras Gregorian (old gothic), Vuelo (nu-disco after Flight Facilities), Hollywood (Lana Del Rey's beats under old-Hollywood strings)
    Soul R&B (after Jorja Smith and Snoh Aalegra), Droon (after Sonder)
    Solos Solos (ballads after Coltrane's Ballads and Chet Baker), Bebop (fast horn lines after Parker, Coltrane and Baker)
    Others Jazz, and Piig, a smaller style pack

    Most packs have five presets each for the drums, the bass, the lead and the chords. Solos and Bebop have six, and Piig has three.

    Play a pack

    1. Copy the pack's ravving folder to the root of a USB stick.
    2. Plug the stick into your MIKO.
    3. Open a preset dialog and pick SOURCE · USB. The presets play straight from the stick.

    The USB page can also import them, so they stay on the device.

    The notes follow your key

    The notes are written as scale degrees, so a pack plays in whatever key and scale you set. Each pack sticks to one scale, so its four instruments play together:

    • Natural Minor: Hip-Hop, Trap, Reggaeton, Piig, Heavy Metal, Black Metal, Post-Punk, Vuelo, R&B, Droon, Hollywood.
    • Major: Classical, Pop, Rock, Punk, Tropical, Cumbia, Folk, Solos, Bebop.
    • Dorian: Gregorian.

    Tropical's minor styles (salsa, bachata) and Cumbia's (villera, chicha) sit on degree 6, the relative minor. That keeps each pack in one key: in C major, they play in A minor.

    Notes from outside the scale use ALTER, like rock's ♭3 and ♭7 or trap's ♭2. Chords from outside the key use chromatic voicings.

    Tempo and swing

    A pattern doesn't store its tempo or swing. The tables give each preset's tempo in quarter-note BPM on the 16-step grid. Every preset plays straight (SWING 50), except these:

    Feel: velocity, nudge and length

    Notes can carry their own velocity and nudge, like SELECT's knobs 2 and 3 set them. The packs use that for feel:

    • palm-muted chugs and tremolo strokes,
    • a fiddle's bow and a chant's swell,
    • grace notes snapped in early,
    • a dub bass sitting behind the beat.

    3/4 and 6/8

    Some presets are in 3/4 or 6/8, like Classical's waltz trio (Waltz, Waltz Oom and Waltz Pah-Pah). Three 16-step bars hold four 12-step bars, so they loop together. The tempo counts a quarter as four steps, so a jig at a dotted quarter of 110 reads 165.

    Making a pack

    Each pack is built by a script, and a checker tests every pack against ravving's own readers.

    data/secret/<genre>-presets/build.py                     the generator (python3 build.py)
data/secret/<genre>-presets/ravving/presets/<drums|bass|lead|chords>/patterns/<Genre> · <Name>.json
data/secret/patternkit.py                                shared by the genre build.py files (not the jazz one)
data/secret/validate.py                                  checks every pack against ravving's parsers

    Each preset file is {"ravving": "preset", "type": "pattern", "library": "<x>-presets.json", "preset": {"name", "genre", "chart", "sound"}}.

    • chart: the pattern, in the shipped libraries' format.
    • sound: the sound it was written for. That's {"soundSource": …} for the bass, lead and chords, and {"kit": …} for the drums.
    • Velocity and nudge: patternkit writes them from a note's @64 and <10 / >10 tokens (10 % of a step early or late). They land in the chart's VELOCITY and NUDGE lanes.
    • Length: a note's _ steps, in the chart's HOLD lane.
    • Templates: pack.template(…) writes ravving/presets/templates/<genre>.json, and a second one under its own name=.

    The stick's presets reach the dialog through PresetExchange::stickPatterns and loadStickPattern.

    Sounds

    Every preset says which sound it was written for, and loads it when WITH ITS SOUND is on. All of them are already on your MIKO: nothing needs installing.

    • Drums: the drum machine kits, plus acoustic kits picked from a General MIDI soundfont. Some, like the orchestra, cumbia and Latin kits, map their rows to other instruments: the orchestra kit's TOM and HAT are timpani, and the Latin kits swap in congas, bongos, güiro and cowbell.
    • Bass, lead and chords: soundfont instruments, and "808 · Monosynth", a sub bass on Calf Monosynth that glides between notes.

    Drum kits don't follow the key, so the orchestra kit's timpani sound best in C.

    Soundfont sounds don't glide: a SLIDE only overlaps the notes. That's why the 808 lines use "808 · Monosynth".

    Solos: Major (ballads after Coltrane's Ballads and Chet Baker)

    Made for Eb major, a tenor's ballad key. The minor moods sit on degree 6 (C minor), and Devotion vamps on degree 2 (F Dorian).

    The lead's degree 1 is Eb4, so each horn has its own range:

    Horn Range
    Tenor "5-" to "1+", crying up to "2+"
    Soprano "5" to "3+"
    Trumpet "7-" to "6"

    Six original ballad solos for the LEAD, each with the brushes, bass and chords it's heard over. They're moody, long-breathed and passionate, with no fast passages.

    The model is the John Coltrane Quartet's Ballads (Impulse!, 1962–63, with Tyner, Garrison and Jones), plus Chet Baker's slow trumpet. Nothing is quoted: the album's standards and the recorded solos are copyrighted. Every line, progression and rhythm is new.

    The playing

    • Tempo 56–70.
    • Long held notes, 6–12 steps, with a beat or half a bar of air between phrases.
    • A swell written across each phrase in velocities: @60 rising to @100–110 and falling back. The MODS soften each note's attack (ATTACK +2400 timecents on the sax, +3600 on the trumpet) and lengthen the release.
    • Every long note sits 8–16 % of a step late, the rubato lean.
    • Flurries only as ornaments: three or four sixteenths pushed early and climbing into a long note.
    • A scoop or slur (~) into a long tone.

    The band

    • Chords: Tyner-style rootless 9ths and 13ths, So What fourths, fourths planed over a pedal, Lydian clusters, the minor iv (Abm6) and the backdoor Db9. Each is held a bar or half a bar, then re-voiced.
    • Bass: Garrison's two-feel, in whole notes and pedals.
    • Drums: brushes only, darkened with TONE and CUTOFF, with a crash rolled into the downbeat.

    The playing

    SWING works on the EIGHTHS here, and the templates set it. The ride's "ding ding-a" and the bebop eighths sit two steps apart on the 16-step grid.

    • Parker: lines in eighths on the chord tones, with chromatic approach notes and enclosures. The off-beats are a touch louder, the approach notes snap in early, and the blue ♭3 scoops into the 3. A motif is stated, then displaced a beat, with a breath between phrases.
    • Coltrane: sheets of sound, sixteenth 1-2-3-5 cells up and their mirror down on every chord, each run swelling to its top. The modal solo, on soprano, is pentatonic cells and stacked fourths up to the Eb6, with a high note cried four times.
    • Baker: few notes, long and soft, every one behind the beat, with a scoop and room to breathe. The trumpet's cutoff is down a little and its release longer.

    Hollywood: Natural Minor (Lana Del Rey under old-Hollywood strings)

    Made for F minor, the key of Blue Jeans and Honeymoon. The contrabass's and cello's low F and C sit under the strings, and the orchestra kit's timpani reach F.

    Lana Del Rey's beats and progressions, from Born to Die to Norman Fucking Rockwell!, with an old-Hollywood orchestra: violins, cellos, string sections, harp, celesta and timpani. Vintage, cinematic and melancholic.

    The songs

    Her progressions come from the chord sheets, set on the scale's degrees:

    • Video Games' i III v VI (F#m A C#m D).
    • Born to Die's VI i VII VI (C Em D C).
    • Blue Jeans' i VII IV (Fm Eb Bb, the major IV a chromatic chord here).
    • Summertime Sadness' VI i VII iv (A C#m B F#m).
    • Honeymoon's i VI iv6 V7, in three.
    • Venice Bitch's minor plagal C C7 F Fm6, in the relative major (III III7 VI vi6).

    The old-Hollywood scoring adds the minor line cliché (Fm, Fm(maj7), Fm7, Fm6, the Bond theme's "suspense motif") with the lament bass walking down under it, the half-step sigh, tremolo strings swelling, and harp sweeps. Nothing is quoted: her songs are copyrighted, so the style was studied (tools/genre-research/hollywood.md) and every line, melody and rhythm is new.

    The playing

    • Slow: 56–88, the half-time beats written at their felt tempo.
    • Velocity swells across each bowed phrase, and long holds.
    • The strings and the cellos 6–10 % of a step late.
    • A written tremolo: sixteenths of one note swelling from @40 to @104 and back.
    • Harp sweeps rising in velocity to a held top note.
    • The celesta's grace notes snapped in early (<12), and slides on the 808 sub.

    The MODS shape the sound:

    • the strings' ATTACK slowed (2400–4800) and their RELEASE lengthened, for the hall;
    • the FILTER pulled down, for the vintage tone;
    • a little VIBRATO on the solo strings;
    • the drum kits darkened (TONE), and the SP1200 tuned down two semitones.
