    Using ravving / docs/manual-interim.md

    The manual

    The five pages and what plays on them, the shortcuts, undo, sound sources, presets, templates, sections and the song, exports, LIVE REC, files, USB sticks, Bluetooth, the RECORDER, the issues center.

    This is the interim manual: what each page, key and dialog does. A friendlier guide with screenshots is on its way.

    Each of the five left-column number keys opens a page:

    Key Page Machines What plays (the nameplate shows it)
    1 Drums (press 1 again to cycle) DRUMS 1 · 2 · 3 FluidSynth with a drum kit each: Roland TR909, Roland TR808 and LINN9000 to start, any kit from the device's SFZ Drum Machines folder from the nameplate
    2 Chords CHORDS TAL NoiseMaker (jalv) with its factory sounds, or a soundfont instrument (Rhodes, vibes, organ, strings…)
    3 Bass BASS DISTRHO Nekobi (jalv), or a soundfont bass (upright, acoustic, finger, fretless, synth)
    4 Lead LEAD Calf Monosynth (jalv), or a soundfont instrument (Rhodes, vibes, guitar, sax, flute…)
    5 Sampler (press 5 again to switch) SAMPLE KING · PAD SAMPLER a sampler: four banks of eight slots, or sixteen MPC-style pads on the step keys with takes, pad FX, 16 levels and a shared wave editor

    All five share the top bar: key and scale, transport, tempo and swing, live FX, the instruments strip and master; every machine has a mix strip, SOUND MODIFIERS, its pattern editor and a SOUND EFFECTS chain; a song is made of SECTIONS that change on the bar line, and the whole session lives in projects.

    Installing

    ravving comes from the Zynthbox Store's Apps category as an AppImage. The AppImage opens a setup page over Zynthbox with four choices: TRY IT, MAKE IT THE DEFAULT, ZYNTHBOX AT BOOT AGAIN, REMOVE RAVVING. Projects, takes and recordings stay when ravving is removed.

    Playing and editing

    Shortcuts you'll use all the time

    Press What happens
    ALT + ▲ Jump to the header, from anywhere
    ALT + step keys 1–8 Switch the LIVE FX on and off: DROP, NO KICK, ONLY KICK, CRUSH, LO PASS, HI PASS, STUTTER, LOOP
    ALT + knobs 1–3 PHASER, ECHO and HEAVY
    ALT + big knob Master volume
    MODE The instrument on screen goes OFF → LIVE → STEP + LIVE → OFF
    STOP, while stopped Panic: every held note ends
    Long press on a machine's S Solo that machine alone

    While you hold ALT, the step keys light up orange and the LIVE FX pads show their key numbers.

    Play it live

    Turn LIVE on with MODE, and the step keys become something you can play:

    • Bass, lead and the chords' NOTES tab: the scale is spread over all 16 keys. ▲ ▼ shift the octave.
    • The chords' CHORDS and SLOTS tabs: you get the seven chord keys of your key.

    Write notes step by step

    STEP entry writes what you play at a cursor.

    • ◀ ▶ move the cursor a step.
    • Keys you hold together land in the same step, so a chord goes into one column.
    • With the transport stopped, SELECT writes the selected row at the cursor: a note, or the chord on CHORDS.
    • With both LIVE and STEP on, ▲ ▼ walk the rows instead of the octave. Each row sounds as you reach it, so you can find the note by ear first.

    Touch the grid

    • One finger drags sideways to page through the bars. On a piano roll it also drags the rows up and down; past eight semitones, the octave shifts.
    • Two fingers pinch to zoom.
    • A tap still toggles a note.

    The pointer only shows while a mouse is plugged in.

    Save and open projects

    • The first SAVE asks for the project's name, and the on-screen keyboard comes up.
    • Later saves write over it. Press SAVE twice within three seconds (it asks AGAIN?).
    • SAVED means nothing has changed since; the button wakes up with your next edit.
    • SAVE AS NEW asks for a new name.

    PROJECT ▾ lists your projects and the demos. Tap one to open it. If you have unsaved changes, tap it a second time to confirm. A long press or ★ makes a project a favourite.

    The row at the bottom always works on the project you have open: NEW, TEMPLATES and IMPORT, then SAVE AS NEW, then EXPORT.

    Undo

    The header's ↶ ↷ step the whole project back and forth. ALT + ◀ ▶ do the same on the machine pages. A change that touched several machines at once counts as one step: CLEAR ALL, or a key change that moved both the bass and the lead.

    A machine's own ↶ ↷, in its toolbar, undo only that machine's changes.

    What comes back:

    • Patterns and slots.
    • The arrangement: a removed section with its content, a rename, a colour, a length, and edits to the song.

    What doesn't: sounds, the mix and the project's settings. Picking a section isn't a step either. More in Undo and redo.

    LOCK: keep a stray tap from changing what plays

    LOCK is the first button of every pattern toolbar. While it's on, the note grids don't write anything. Taps on cells, step keys, STEP entry, SELECT's moves and the note knobs are all refused, and LOCK flashes to tell you why. You can still scroll, page and listen.

    • PLAY turns LOCK on. STOP turns it off again.
    • Tap LOCK while playing to edit anyway. It then stays unlocked after you stop.
    • Tap LOCK while stopped to lock the grids. It then stays locked.

    There's one LOCK for every page, and a take (●) still records.

    SELECT: move notes after you've written them

    SELECT sits in the pattern toolbar of the bass, lead and chords, beside ♭♯. While it's on, a tap on the grid picks a note instead of writing one. Tap again to unpick it. A tap on a held note's tail picks that note.

    • ▲ ▼ move every picked note one note of the key up or down. With ♭♯ on, they move a semitone. Length, accent and slide come along.
    • Each move is one undo step.
    • On the chords page, the CHORDS view picks chords and moves them a degree. The NOTES view picks single notes. A held chord always moves as one.
    • A long press on SELECT picks every note (or chord) in all the bars, so you can move the whole pattern at once.
    • Turning SELECT off drops the picks.

    Shape a single note

    On the bass and lead, pick notes with SELECT, then turn knobs 1–3 to change them together. A readout at the top right of the grid shows the first picked note's values, with the knob badges.

    Knob Setting Range One detent
    1 LENGTH (the lighter cells) 1 step to 4 bars a step
    2 VELOCITY 1–127 4
    3 NUDGE, from where the swing puts the note half a step early to half a step late a 16th of a step
    • A long press on a cell also sets a note's length.
    • The ACCENT lane's cell fills to the velocity, bright red when accented. A tap there still flips the accent: full, or back to the plain velocity.
    • A LIVE take with Q off records a nudge for each hit.
    • These settings move with the note, each detent is one undo step, and they're kept with the project and in a saved pattern preset.

    The big knob stays the volume, and a focused group of dials keeps its own knobs.

    SLOTS: write with whole chords

    On the chords page, SLOTS lets you compose chord by chord:

    1. Tap a slot.
    2. Play a chord key. The next slot arms itself.
    3. Set each slot's length: 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars. All slots together never pass the pattern's eight bars; the "n / 8 BARS" readout keeps count.

    The slots write the chord pattern, and PLAY plays them in order with the current slot lit. ＋ adds four more slots, up to eight, and − takes them away.

    If you change the pattern in the CHORDS or NOTES view, the view says PATTERN CHANGED and dims the slots. Editing a slot then rebuilds the pattern from the slots. Undo brings your edits back.

    SAMPLE KING

    Page 5 holds two samplers. Press 5 again to swap between SAMPLE KING and the PAD SAMPLER; ravving remembers which one you used.

    SAMPLE KING gives you four banks (A to D) of eight slots, and each bank has its own pattern. The bank on screen is the one that plays.

    • Get a sound in. Pick a slot (its row in the grid) and hit LOAD to choose one of your samples. Or record one: ● REC records the device's input straight into the slot, ● again stops, and the take lands there trimmed to its last burst of sound. INPUT sets up what's plugged in, the gain and the monitor.
    • Shape it. Drag the start and end handles on the waveform (or use knobs 1 and 2 once the waveform has the focus), set PITCH up to 12 semitones up or down, and flip REVERSE to play it backwards. A slot plays one-shot: hitting it again cuts the last hit off.
    • Chop it. CHOP and AUTO CHOP open the wave editor and spread the pieces over a bank's slots (see the wave editor below).
    • Play it. Each row of the step grid is a slot, with an accent row under them, and knobs 1–3 set the selected row's mute, solo and level, just like the drums. Turn LIVE on and step keys 1–8 play the slots, with key 9 held for accents. A MIDI keyboard plays them too.

    The PAD SAMPLER

    Sixteen pads in four banks (A to D), laid out like an MPC. Pick a pad on screen and its sample, its tools and its settings show up beside the pads; its 16 steps sit underneath.

    • Get a sound in. LOAD picks a sample for the pad. ● REC records the input into it; with SMART on, the take stops by itself once the sound goes quiet, and the pad gets it trimmed to its last burst. COPY copies a pad onto another, CLEAR empties it, and ERASE removes the pad's hits from the pattern.
    • How it plays. NOTE ON plays the pad only while you hold it; with it off, a tap plays the whole sample (one-shot). LOOP loops it from the loop point, REVERSE plays it backwards, and MUTE silences it (UNMUTE ALL brings every pad back).
    • Tweak it. The tabs TRIM, MIX, AMP ENV, TUNE, PLAY and FILTER each put three settings on knobs 1–3, and ◀ ▶ switch tabs. That's the start and end, the volume and pan, the attack and decay, the tuning (semitones, then fine), mono or poly with a mute group, and a filter with its type, cutoff and resonance.
    • Play it live. Turn LIVE on and the 16 step keys become the 16 pads. A tap plays a pad; hold one for half a second and it latches, looping until you tap it again. STOP ends every latched loop.
    • PAD FX. While a pad plays, hold ECHO, TAPE STOP, SCRATCH, STUTTER, SKIP or REVERSE to throw an effect on it. Let go and it's back to normal.
    • 16 LEVELS. Switch 16 LVL on and all sixteen pads play the pad you had selected, spread by TYPE: VELOCITY (soft to hard), TUNE (a semitone per pad) or SCALE (up your project's scale). The kit's SOUND MODIFIERS set the kit's volume and tuning for every pad.

    The wave editor

    Both samplers share it. CHOP cuts a sample into pieces three ways: REGIONS (equal slices), THRESHOLD (at the hits louder than a level) and MANUAL (tap the wave to add a cut, tap a cut to remove it). AUTO CHOP opens it straight on the hits. ◀ ▶ change the chop type, the big knob sets the slices or the threshold, NORMALISE raises the sample to full level, TRIM cuts the silence at both ends, and APPLY sends the pieces to a bank's slots or to the pads.

    Undo and redo

    The project has one history. The header's ↶ ↷ (and ALT + ◀ ▶ on the machine pages) walk it back and forth, and a toast names each step ("UNDO · CHORDS + BASS"). A machine's own ↶ ↷ on its pattern toolbar undo that machine's last change only, wherever it is in the history.

    • What is undone: every machine's notes, steps, chords, slots, accents, slides, lengths, automation and live timing, and the arrangement: which sections there are, their order, names, colours and lengths, and the song. A section removed comes back whole, in its place.
    • What is not: the sounds, the mix, the effects and the project's settings (tempo, swing, key). Picking a section is not a step either.
    • One step: a change that touches several machines at once (CLEAR ALL, a key change that snaps the bass and the lead) is one step; so are quick changes in a row (a knob turning, a handle dragged) and a whole recorded take.
    • The history keeps the last 100 steps, and starts afresh when a project is opened or a new one begins.

    Sound sources

    Bass, lead and chords can sound like something other than their synth: a tap on the nameplate opens the machine's sounds, its own engine first (the 303, Calf Monosynth, TAL NoiseMaker and its factory sounds) and then soundfont instruments played by FluidSynth (an upright and other basses, Rhodes, vibes, organ, strings, sax, flute…, from the device's General MIDI and instrument soundfonts). Choosing one stops the engine and starts the other in its place; a soundfont source has SOUND MODIFIERS of its own (FILTER, ENVELOPE, MOTION). The choice is kept with the project. Acoustic sources for jazz and the like: Grand piano (Fazioli) and Upright piano (Kawai) and a Jazz guitar on CHORDS, Alto and Tenor sax and a Grand piano on LEAD, the Upright on BASS.

    The drum machines' kit picker has an ACOUSTIC group: General MIDI kits picked inside FluidR3's font (Jazz and Brush with the ride and the hi-hat foot on their rows, or with the crash; Standard, Room). Bass and lead notes can last longer than a step (a long press on a cell stretches the row's note up to it), and the header's swing, tapped on its caption, swings the 8ths as jazz does (SWING 8) or the 16ths (SWING 16).

    Pattern presets with their sound and MODS. SAVE CURRENT AS NEW in the presets dialog (PRESET on a pattern toolbar) keeps the machine's sound with the pattern, its source or factory sound, its MODS, or for the drums the kit and the kit's MODS. SAVE PATTERN AS has WITH ITS SOUND (the source or kit playing, its factory sound) and WITH ITS MODS (the MODS that are set: one nobody turned is left out), both on each time it opens; both off, the pattern alone. A curated pack may name just a source or a kit, or add MODS too. Such presets say ♪ and the sound's name under their genre, "+ MODS" when they keep MODS, and the dialog then shows WITH ITS SOUND and WITH ITS MODS (off by default, remembered in the profile under presets/withSound and presets/withMods): loading one also switches the machine to its sound, and sets its MODS, only the parts the preset names (as MY SOUNDS does): a preset with no MODS leaves the machine's MODS as they are. Turning SOUND on turns MODS on too (a sound is heard with its MODS); MODS then turns off on its own, and on alone it brings a preset's MODS to the sound already playing. Both off, only the pattern loads. A source or kit this device does not have leaves that part of the sound as it is. The samplers' presets keep no sound (theirs is in their banks), nor do the shipped ones; a preset copied to a stick carries its sound in its file. Beside it, WITH ITS BARS (on by default, presets/withBars): off, a preset keeps the pattern's bar count instead of bringing its own, laid over it from the first bar (a shorter preset repeats to fill it, a longer one is cut), so the bar pages stay as they are. The presets dialog is organised as the sound pickers are: a GROUP chip per genre, SORT (as listed / A–Z) and ★ favourites (a long press, the ★ at a preset's corner, ALT + SELECT), kept per instrument by the preset's name, so a stick's preset and the same one taken in share their ★; it opens on ALL when the group last shown would hide the preset on screen.

    Templates. PROJECT ▾ → TEMPLATES (beside NEW) lists project templates: a template is how a project starts, its tempo, swing, key (root and scale) and LIVE FX knobs (PHASER, ECHO, HEAVY). SOURCE picks the device's or a USB stick's (read straight from its ravving/presets/templates/ folder), as the pattern presets' dialog does. Choosing one is NEW with those set (asking first over unsaved changes, as NEW does). SAVE TEMPLATE keeps the session's own as one of the device's (DELETE removes it); the USB dialog's PRESETS copies templates either way. Every genre pack ships one beside its patterns (or more: the Vuelo pack has one per key).

    A new project starts clean. NEW, and NEW from a template, put every page back to its defaults: an empty pattern on each machine (no preset loaded), its own engine and the default sound, mix, MODS and SOUND EFFECTS, one section "A", the LIVE FX at rest; a template sets only its tempo, swing, key and LIVE FX knobs on top. The shipped demo patterns come back only through a project or a preset.

    Demos. Ravving ships three projects to open from PROJECT ▾ → DEMOS: Autumn Leaves (a jazz trio and alto sax at 146 BPM, swung 8ths, sections A1 A2 B C and a solo chorus) and 5AM (dark electronic pop at 188 BPM in D harmonic minor over Dm – Gm – A – Dm, the changes of Grimes' "4ÆM", everything else written for ravving: a 909 two-step on a breakbeat, an acid bass, a pumping pad and a lead hook, in six sections with a four-bar HUSH before the last drop) and B3rlin (dark techno at 135 BPM in G Phrygian: a 909 floor with light layers of drive on the kick, the 909's ride and a rim on a three-bar loop, the 808's toms on a five-bar one, a sidechained bass rumble, a filtered lead sequence falling a minor second, a sinister pad and a stab; INTRO · RUMBLE · RIDE · PEAK ×2 · BREAK (no kick, the bass on, a snare roll) · PEAK ×2 · OUTRO). They are installed read-only with ravving and use only the image's own soundfonts, so they play the same on any Zynthbox. A demo opens like a project; SAVE keeps the changes as a project of the user's under the demo's name, the demo itself never changes, and it cannot be deleted.

    Sections and the song

    A song is more than one loop. A section holds every machine's pattern at once (an intro, a verse, a bridge) and which machines rest in it; the sounds, effects, levels, tempo and key stay the project's, so a change of section never restarts an engine. Sections are live: the one on screen is the one you edit, and what you edit stays in it when you pick another.

    • LOOP | ONCE (on each machine's strip, beside ❚❚, kept per section like it). A section lasts as long as its longest pattern, or its LENGTH. A shorter pattern LOOPs through it by default (2 bars of drums play twice under a 4-bar lead); on ONCE it plays its own bars once and rests until the section starts again. The rest is exact: the machine's clip is told to play the section's length, the bars past its own kept empty, so nothing is cut and a tail rings out. While it rests no playhead shows and a hit played live is heard, not written.

    • When a switch lands: SWITCH · BAR | SECTION (the SONG page, kept with the project). BAR: a section picked while playing takes over on the next bar. SECTION: it waits for the section playing to finish its pass (its key blinks, the one playing stays lit; picking the one playing again lets it go; stopping plays it at once), then switches on that pass's bar line, in LOOP and in SONG (the song jumps to the picked section's entry). A one-bar section ends every bar. In LOOP the picks queue up, up to three: each plays one pass, then the next takes over, the last looping on; the keys wear their place (1 blinking: next), a queued key picked again leaves the queue, the one playing picked empties it, a fourth pick is refused (QUEUE FULL), stopping puts the first in place and drops the rest. In SONG one pick waits, a new one taking its place. A switch always lands on a bar line, never on a beat.

    • Switching on the bar line. A section picked while the transport plays takes over on the next bar, sample-accurately, so no note drops or doubles (a machine whose part does not change keeps playing). Stopped, a pick is immediate.
    • The plate. The header's project plate wears the section on screen as a square key with its number, in its colour, blinking while it waits for the bar; a tap opens the SONG page.
    • X and Y (the two keys beside the big knob) go to the previous and the next section from any page or dialog, wrapping round, as a section key would pick it (from the next bar while playing, at once when stopped); pressed again before the switch lands, they move the pick on rather than queue another. With one section a toast says to add more on the SONG page.
    • ALT + step keys 9–16 pick sections 1–8 from any page (lit in their colours while ALT is held; ALT + 1–8 stay the LIVE FX pads). With the section keys in the header, ALT + 1–16 pick sections 1–16, and while their group has the focus (the header's ◀ ▶ onto it) the step keys alone pick them, lit in the sections' colours.
    • The section keys in the header. A long press on the project plate, or ALT + MODE (MODE lights orange while ALT is held; MODE alone still cycles the entry mode), puts the section keys where the LIVE FX pads are (the same again brings the pads back): the SONG page's keys, at the pads' size, without their dots. A tap picks one (on the next bar while it plays), ▶ marks the one sounding and its progress fills it, a swipe up removes one (it follows the finger and fades; past half its height it flies off; never the last), more than fit swipe sideways (the strip slides to the section that becomes current or that the song reaches), the keys grouped on a slab in the song's colour with ＋ pinned at its end, always in reach.
    • Seeing the change. As the section sounding changes (a SONG part, or a LOOP pick landing on the bar line) the plate sweeps in its colour and the instrument page glows round its frame in it, once; the section keys (here and on the SONG page) fill with the section's progress.
    • The SONG page, under the header like the MIXER (ALT + 1 from anywhere, again to go back), wears its own nameplate as an instrument does and lays the sections out at a finger's size, in a frame as the instruments' grids: square keys wearing their number with the name under (eight a row, the first row being the ALT keys), a dot per sounding machine; tap picks, double tap renames, hold and drag moves, × deletes (a second tap), ＋ copies the section on screen (A → A′). Under them: RENAME, NEW (empty), COLOUR, LENGTH (1–8 bars or AUTO, the longest pattern), MOVE, ADD TO SONG. The song is a strip of blocks as wide as their bars × repeats: a block picked moves, repeats (×1–×16) or goes. Keys: ◀ ▶ walk, ▲ ▼ between keys and blocks, SELECT or a step key picks, ALT + ◀ ▶ moves, knob 1 the length, knob 2 the repeats, BACK leaves.

    SONG mode. LOOP | SONG on the SONG page says what PLAY plays: the section on screen, looped, or the song through. In SONG the first part's section is in place before the first note, each next part is cued as the last bar of the one before begins, and the song stops on the bar line after its end; the block playing shows its progress and the plate the part ("6/9"). While the song plays the instrument pages follow it, their note grids locked as whenever the music plays (LOCK, below); unlocked, an edit goes into the section playing. ▶ PLAY FROM HERE starts it at any block; a section picked by hand while it plays jumps to its first part (one the song does not have goes back to LOOP). EXPORT → WHAT: SONG records the whole song in one take, its own length plus the tail; LOOP is BARS × LOOPS.

    Up to 16 sections, saved with the project.

    CHORDS also carries the image's pad soundfonts, grouped by name in its picker: Strings (Ensemble, Fat, Digital, Rise, Bowed, Piano), Organ (Phaser, Pipe 16+8+4, House, Extreme) and Alien (the "Ambients & Drones" fonts: The Thing, Strange voices, Crystal voice, Glassy, Winds, Chaos, The Pulse), each from the device's own soundfonts. A sustained, polyphonic machine suits them: the chords hold while the voicing lasts, and the key keeps them in tune with the bass and lead. The sound pickers group their lists (GROUP chips, ★ for the favourites you mark with the star or ALT + SELECT, SORT as listed or A–Z) and remember the group and sort per instrument.

    Languages

    ravving speaks English, Spanish and Japanese, with the words musicians use in each (BATERÍA, BOMBO, COMPÁS; ドラム, キック, 小節). MENU → SETTINGS → LANGUAGE: SYSTEM (the default) follows the device's own language, or pick English, Español or 日本語; the screen changes at once. The keys printed on the device (MENU, ALT, SELECT…) and the machines' names stay as printed. The webpage has Spanish and Japanese pages too (the switcher in its top bar); the docs are in English.

    Startup splash

    ravving shows its logo while it starts, until the page is ready, then plays a short show into the page. MENU → SETTINGS → STARTUP picks the look, from the next start: ANIME (fast cuts through the five flow-lens logos into the glow one), GLOW (one at random, its halo swelling), STILL (one at random, a fade), ZOOM (the fluid logo rushing out past the screen) or OFF. While the studio loads, the splash is a still picture, so the start stays quick.

    Exports and the music player

    PROJECT ▾ → EXPORT writes the project out of the device, into the library's exports/ folder:

    • A track: the whole production as the speakers hear it, recorded while the project plays from its first beat for BARS × LOOPS bars plus a TAIL for the echoes, cut to the sample, in WAV 24-bit, FLAC, OGG Vorbis or MP3, with its tags (title, artist, album / mixtape, genre, composer, comment, date, BPM), a cover drawn for it (from the title and the artist, with MADE WITH RAVVING) and a JSON sidecar. The master's level is kept as it is: a track that peaks over full scale is pulled down to −0.1 dBFS and the sidecar says so.
    • A bundle: a .ravv zip of the project with every sample it uses, for another ravving to play it the same; PROJECT ▾ → IMPORT lists the bundles found in exports/ (and the library's root, where a transfer lands) and makes a project of one.

    The ARTIST and COMPOSER start from the profile's author (MENU → SETTINGS: USER and AUTHOR); the ALBUM and GENRE last typed are offered again.

    MENU → SETTINGS → INTERACTIONS: NOTES OUTSIDE THE SCALE (on by default). On, a key played outside the key (a MIDI keyboard's) on the lead, the bass or the chords' NOTES sounds and records in a row of its own on the roll; off, it is muted there and never recorded. The drums, the samplers and the chords played on a degree ignore it.

    Video recordings and the computer link

    The control center's LIVE REC card records the session: a tap opens a dialog that asks WHAT, a VIDEO (the screen or a USB camera with the master's sound, an MP4 in the library's videos/ folder) or AUDIO ONLY (the sound alone, a 24-bit WAV among the TRACKS in the exports folder), whether the MIC is in, and START (the dialog is the confirmation, so a stray tap never starts a recording; ending one is a single tap; a new project switches LIVE REC off). While LIVE REC is on, the device's RECORD key starts a recording and STOP ends it (the music goes on), from any page; SETTINGS · VIDEO has the QUALITY (three tiers, none of which costs the sound a thing), the TIMER counted down before a recording, the SOURCE, MIC (the RECORDER's input in the video's sound) and VOICE, the voice-over mix: while the mic carries a voice the music ducks under it (DUCK −6 / −12 / −18 dB, −12 by default) and the voice is lifted (+6 / +12 dB) and made crisp (a low cut and a presence lift), so a voice over the playing is heard on top rather than buried; a voice is the mic 12 dB over what the speakers are heard to leak into it (the beat does not duck itself), the mix goes through a look-ahead limiter rather than clipping, and the mic's LEVEL is metered from the moment VIDEO is on (in SETTINGS and standing on the project plate; MIC TOO HOT when its peaks pass −6 dBFS: turn the RECORDER's GAIN down); with the RECORDER on GUITAR or LINE the input is summed as it is. Both apply to a recording under way. VIDEO SAVED or LIVE REC SAVED, when one ends, offers DELETE at its left (a second tap within three seconds confirms), RENAME (the file keeps its extension) and SEND… through one dialog that lists every USB stick, every computer and the phone; an audio recording also has METADATA…, the export dialog over the file, which tags it (title, artist, album, genre, composer, comment), draws its cover, writes it in the format chosen (WAV, FLAC, OGG, MP3) and sends it on, through the track export's own finishing; the same SEND… is on the music player's tracks, in FILES and in the presets dialogs, and the export dialog can send a new track or bundle to a computer as it is written.

    SETTINGS · COMPUTERS holds any number of computers (laptops or PCs on the network, over SSH): ADD A COMPUTER asks for its host, user and password, the password is used once to install the device's one key there, and files land in that computer's Downloads folder; each has CHANGE (set up again, TEST, FORGET).

    Sending to a phone

    Every SEND… button (the FILES browser, VIDEO SAVED, the music player's tracks, the presets dialogs, the sound picker) has a third destination beside the USB sticks and the computer: PHONE · QR CODE. It opens a dialog with a QR code of a link to the file, served by ravving itself at a random, unguessable address; point the phone's camera app at the screen, open the link it shows, and Safari or Chrome saves the file to the phone's Downloads (Files on an iPhone). The phone must be on the same Wi-Fi as the device (the link is the device's Wi-Fi address; the dialog says when the device is on no network); no pairing, no app and no account are needed, which makes it the way to an iPhone (Bluetooth's file push does not exist there). The link lives while the dialog is open, 15 minutes at most; closing it stops the server. One file at a time: given several, the first one goes and the dialog says so. The dialog shows the link in words too (to type it), and WAITING FOR THE PHONE / SENDING n % / DOWNLOADED ✓ as it goes; a toast says when the whole file has reached the phone.

    The same server carries the ravving page (the instructions, the features and every hardware shortcut): SETTINGS → HELP → OPEN shows its address as a QR code (http://<device>:8098/help/), since the device has no browser of its own; a phone's camera opens it, a laptop types it. The same page is public at https://ravving.github.io/ (the dialog's QR code is that one, reachable from anywhere; the device's own address is under it for a Wi-Fi without internet).

    Files

    The control center's FILES card browses everything in the library by kind (projects, templates, presets, samples, takes, the RECORDER's takes, recordings, tracks, bundles, soundfonts, MIDI files, videos, received files), newest first, each entry with SEND… (the standard dialog: a USB stick's ravving folder, one of the computers or the phone) and DELETE. It is the one place files leave the device or are deleted; the PROJECT dialog opens, saves and exports, and the USB page brings a stick's files in. PHONE · BROWSE in its header shows a QR code of the same library as pages for a phone (or a laptop) on the Wi-Fi: every kind with its count, each kind's files with their detail, size and date, and a DOWNLOAD per file, served by ravving itself (the link lives until 15 minutes pass with no request from the phone, the dialog closed or not); a project or anything that is a folder is listed as "on the device"; a link that ran out says so on the phone.

    USB sticks

    A stick plugged in shows up as a USB row in the export dialog (a Zynthbox runs udisks but mounts nothing by itself; ravving mounts the stick when it needs it): the track or bundle is written to the library as always and copied to the stick's ravving/ exports/ or ravving/projects/ folder, then EJECT (in the dialog or on the USB page) flushes, unmounts and powers it off so it can be pulled. To put a saved project on a stick, send it from FILES (SEND… bundles it for the stick); PROJECT ▾ → IMPORT lists the bundles found on every stick (its ravving/projects/ and its root) beside the device's own. The project dialog also keeps favourites (★, a long press).

    The drawer's USB card opens the USB page: ON USB lists what the stick holds by kind (SAMPLES, SOUNDFONTS, PROJECTS, TRACKS, MIDI FILES: found anywhere on it, its ravving/<kind> folders first, system folders skipped), each with IMPORT (✓ ON DEVICE once the library has it); FROM DEVICE lists the library's, each with TO USB. Samples land in samples/imported/<stick>/ keeping the stick's folders, soundfonts in soundfonts/, MIDI files in midi/, tracks in exports/ (the music player lists them), projects through the bundle import. One copy at a time, through a part file, a copy onto the stick synced before it counts. The presets dialog (a pattern toolbar's PRESET) has a SOURCE row when a stick is in: DEVICE, or USB · , which lists that machine's pattern presets in the stick's ravving/presets/<drums|bass|lead|chords>/patterns and plays the one picked straight from there, without taking it in (one UNDO puts the pattern back). The dialog opens on the source the pattern on screen came from (the stick a preset was played from, while it is in), with that preset marked. IMPORT ALL beside the folder path brings every new file of the folder on screen and its folders in one go (a sample pack is one tap: IMPORTING 12 / 229 · STOP, one look through the stick and one message at the end; 229 samples in well under a minute). ◀ ▶ the kinds, ALT + ◀ ▶ the side, ▲ ▼ the files, SELECT copies, EJECT in the header. With more than one stick (or partition) plugged in, the card names each stick and a tap asks which one to open. A read-only stick (an installer's ISO image, a locked stick) says so: nothing is offered to copy to it, exports and TO USB skip it, and its page shows FORMAT, which erases the whole stick into one writable FAT32 partition labelled RAVVING (EJECT and FORMAT both want a second press within three seconds).

    A soundfont brought in is used at once: one that looks like a drum kit (three drum roles or more in its sample names) joins the kit picker under IMPORTED, and every preset of every imported font is a chords / bass / lead source, " · ", grouped by font in the sound pickers.

    Bluetooth

    MENU → BLUETOOTH has the switch, SCAN (a short scan of classic devices; it starts on its own only while no speaker plays, since a scan breaks up a playing Bluetooth link on the Pi's shared radio), and VISIBLE (the device can be found for three minutes, so a phone can send it a file); the devices connected first, then paired, then in range. A tap on one opens its row: PAIR (a passkey to confirm or a PIN to type is asked in the head), CONNECT / DISCONNECT, SEND… (an exported track or bundle, picked next; the progress runs along the bottom), PLAY THROUGH for a speaker or headphones (the whole studio's sound switches to it, DEVICE OUTPUT brings it back; the same choice sits in the drawer's AUDIO OUTPUT card with the jacks and HDMI) and FORGET. Files sent to the device land in the library's incoming/ folder: a bundle shows in PROJECT ▾ → IMPORT, a track in the music player under "Received". A stock Zynthbox needs a little setup for Bluetooth, which ravving's setup applies.

    A speaker that does not answer (asleep, waking up, or busy with a phone) is called again three more times over about 35 s (the row reads "waking…"; DISCONNECT stops it) before COULD NOT CONNECT. One audio device plays at a time: connecting a second one while another plays hands the sound over (SWITCHING FROM · the other device, which is disconnected). PLAY THROUGH routes once the connection is made. When Bluetooth comes on (a start, the switch) the output kept is connected again, once, if nothing else plays.

    MENU → MUSIC PLAYER (and DASHBOARD to come back) is a full-screen player for the exported tracks and the ● recordings: TRACKS (sorted), ALBUMS and ARTISTS on the left, the cover, transport, position and volume on the right over the QUEUE. Keys 1 2 3 switch the views, ▲ ▼ pick, SELECT plays, PLAY pauses, STOP stops, the big knob is the volume and knob 1 the position, BACK leaves. Opening the player stops the transport and leaving it stops the player.

    Audio output

    MENU → AUDIO OUTPUT picks where the studio's sound goes: the device's jacks (the default), HDMI, or any Bluetooth speaker or headphones connected in the BLUETOOTH dialog. The sound is glitch-free and comes out of one place only. The choice is kept: when the output goes away (headphones turned off) the sound falls back to the jacks, and it goes back to the output once it is there again (a restart, a reconnection; a Bluetooth output's address is kept with it, so that Bluetooth can connect it when it comes on); picking the jacks forgets it. The dialog's list follows what is plugged in while it is open. The music player, which plays beside the master, follows the output picked too. The drawer's OUTPUT slider (knob 2) sets the amplifier's level on the jacks and the chosen sink's own level otherwise. Each paired speaker or headphones has its own SBC-XQ chip in the BLUETOOTH dialog (open its row): off plays it on plain SBC, sturdier over a weak link, whatever SETTINGS says for the rest; a device without its own choice follows SETTINGS. While another output plays, ravving listens to what it sends there: if the music goes out but nothing plays for six seconds, it routes the sound again once, and if it is still silent the issues center says the output plays no sound.

    The RECORDER

    MENU → RECORDER is a full-screen page (as the music player) for recording the input freely, as played. The transport keeps going, so a take can be played over the beat. The input is a tab (MIC · GUITAR · LINE); under the bar, MONITOR and its LEVEL, the jack lights, the GAIN with the meter, ● REC beside a graph of the incoming level, KEEP TAKES IN (SAMPLES: samples/recordings, the samplers' browsers list them; TRACKS: the library's recordings, the music player lists them) and the EFFECTS made for the input (on GUITAR the amps and pedals: AMP, OVERDRIVE, FUZZ, TUBE, WAH, HOT AMP, from the guitarix LV2s on the image). A take is kept as it came in: no compression, no trim, no SMART stop. On the right the RECORDINGS of both places, newest first: ▶ PLAY, TRIM and NORMALISE (a new recording beside it), TO SAMPLE KING / TO PADS (into a slot or a pad), CHOP… (onto SAMPLE KING's bank or the pads, the samplers' wave editor), TO TRACKS / TO SAMPLES, RENAME, DELETE (twice).

    On GUITAR the page has a TUNER, on the dry input (before the effects): the note heard and its cents on a ±50 scale, the strings of the tuning as buttons. AUTO goes through the strings from the lowest: a string held within 3 cents for 0.8 s is marked ✓ and the next one is up, until ALL STRINGS IN TUNE; MANUAL keeps the string tapped. TUNING picks a tuning (guitar standard, half and full step down, drop D and C, DADGAD, open D, G, E and C; bass 4 and 5 strings, drop D; ukulele GCEA and baritone), A4 the reference (430–450 Hz). The tuner runs only while it is on screen.

    Keys: ◀ ▶ the input, ▲ ▼ the recordings, RECORD a take, SELECT or PLAY hears the recording picked (STOP stops it), the number keys move the knobs between INPUT (1), TUNER (2) and EFFECTS (3), the big knob is the gain, BACK leaves.

    The issues center

    MENU → the ISSUES icon card (beside MUSIC PLAYER, SETTINGS and the USB card) says what is wrong on the device right now, in plain words, with what to do about it: ALL GOOD, or the count lit in the worst severity's colour, and the full list in the dialog, worst first, each with its detail, a suggestion and an action button where one exists (RESTART RAVVING, GDB OFF · RESTART). Every component runs its own check pass and reports: the sound engine missing, an engine that could not start, audio connections that never appeared, audio dropouts (counted every minute), the device's keys dying (zyncoder's GPIO thread, read off stderr), the music player without its PulseAudio bridge, Bluetooth unavailable, an export that failed, a USB stick that would not mount, storage nearly full, the gdb aid left on. New errors and warnings also show as a toast. Under the list, the last warnings ravving printed.

    Where your files live

    ravving keeps to its own folders: it reads the device's sample library and writes only to these places:

    Root On the device What
    config ~/.config/ravving settings (the input set-up, the UI's choices)
    state ~/.local/state/ravving the live session (session.json, the sequence); removed by a purge
    cache ~/.cache/ravving rebuildable: reversed samples, raw takes, the LV2 port cache
    library /zynthian/zynthian-my-data/ravving the user's work, kept across reinstalls: projects/, presets/, samples/ (takes in samples/takes/, the RECORDER's in samples/recordings/, imported bundles' samples in samples/imported/), recordings/ (the master's and the RECORDER's TRACKS), exports/

    On a desktop the library is ~/.local/share/ravving.
