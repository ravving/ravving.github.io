    The manual

    The five pages and what plays on them, the shortcuts, undo, sound sources, presets, templates, sections and the song, exports, LIVE REC, files, USB sticks, Bluetooth, the RECORDER, the issues center.

    This is the interim manual: what each page, key and dialog does. A friendlier guide with screenshots is on its way.

    The five pages

    Hi! Five keys, five pages, eight machines. Let's make some noise.

    Each of the five number keys on the left opens a page:

    Key Page What plays (the nameplate shows it)
    1 DRUMS 1 · 2 · 3 (press 1 again to cycle) A drum kit each: Roland TR909, Roland TR808 and LINN9000 to start, or any kit from the device's drum machines folder
    2 CHORDS TAL NoiseMaker with its factory sounds, or a soundfont instrument (Rhodes, vibes, organ, strings…)
    3 BASS DISTRHO Nekobi, a 303-style bass, or a soundfont bass (upright, acoustic, finger, fretless, synth)
    4 LEAD Calf Monosynth, or a soundfont instrument (Rhodes, vibes, guitar, sax, flute…)
    5 SLOT SAMPLER · PAD SAMPLER (press 5 again to switch) Four banks of eight sample slots, or sixteen MPC-style pads

    All five pages share the top bar: key and scale, transport, tempo and swing, the LIVE FX, the instruments and the master volume.

    Every machine has the same parts:

    • a mix strip;
    • its MODS, the knobs that shape its sound;
    • its pattern editor;
    • a SOUND EFFECTS chain.

    A song is made of SECTIONS that change on the bar line, and your whole session is saved in a project.

    Installing

    ravving comes from the Zynthbox Store's Apps category as an AppImage. The AppImage opens a setup page over Zynthbox with four choices: TRY IT, MAKE IT THE DEFAULT, ZYNTHBOX AT BOOT AGAIN, REMOVE RAVVING. Projects, takes and recordings stay when ravving is removed.

    When the device is missing something ravving needs, the setup page lists it. Tap INSTALL WHAT IS MISSING (it needs the internet), then pick a choice.

    Playing and editing

    Shortcuts you'll use all the time

    Press What happens
    ALT + ▲ Jump to the header, from anywhere
    ALT + step keys 1–16 The LIVE FX pads: the eight in the header, then the eight that take the instruments' place while ALT is held. With the section keys in the header: sections 1–16
    ALT + ▼ The next bank of LIVE FX pads: MAIN, then MORE
    ALT + knobs 1–3 PHASER, ECHO and HEAVY
    ALT + big knob Master volume
    MODE The instrument on screen goes OFF → LIVE → STEP + LIVE → OFF
    ◀ ▶ on a grid The previous / next bar page
    BACK on a grid Undo that machine's last change (ALT + BACK leaves the grid)
    2, on the chords page Turn the tabs: CHORDS → NOTES → SLOTS
    STOP, while stopped Panic: every held note ends
    Long press on a machine's S Solo that machine alone

    While you hold ALT, the step keys light up orange and the LIVE FX pads show their key numbers; a pad shows pressed while its key is down.

    Play it live

    Turn LIVE on with MODE, and the step keys become something you can play:

    • Bass, lead and the chords' NOTES tab: the scale is spread over all 16 keys. ▲ ▼ shift the octave: OCT + / − show pressed and the OCT readout flashes.
    • The chords' CHORDS and SLOTS tabs: you get the seven chord keys of your key.

    Write notes step by step

    STEP entry writes what you play at a cursor.

    • ◀ ▶ move the cursor a column. Past the page's last column, ▶ goes on to the next bar page, and ◀ past the first goes back one. The cursor stops at the pattern's first and last step.
    • Keys you hold together land in the same step, so a chord goes into one column.
    • With the transport stopped, SELECT writes the selected row at the cursor: a note, or the chord on CHORDS.
    • With both LIVE and STEP on, ▲ ▼ walk the rows instead of the octave. Each row sounds as you reach it, so you can find the note by ear first.

    Touch the grid

    • One finger drags sideways to page through the bars. On a piano roll it also drags the rows up and down; past eight semitones, the octave shifts.
    • Two fingers pinch to zoom.
    • A tap still toggles a note.

    The pointer only shows while a mouse is plugged in.

    Bars

    The toolbar's + adds a bar, up to eight. Its − drops the last one: at once when it's empty, or on a second tap when it holds notes (it shows ? while it waits). ‹ › page through the bars, and so do ◀ ▶ on the grid.

    Save and open projects

    • The first SAVE asks for the project's name, and the on-screen keyboard comes up. Tap where the cursor should go, press and hold a word to select it (drag the orange dots to change the selection), and tap ✕ to empty the field. The window rises above the keyboard.
    • Later saves write over it. Press SAVE twice within three seconds (it asks AGAIN?).
    • SAVED means nothing has changed since; the button wakes up with your next edit.
    • SAVE AS NEW asks for a new name.
    • RENAME changes the name and the icon of the project you have open. Your unsaved changes stay unsaved.

    PROJECT ▾ lists your projects and the demos. Tap one to open it. If you have unsaved changes, tap it a second time to confirm. A long press or ★ makes a project a favourite.

    The row at the bottom always works on the project you have open: NEW, TEMPLATES and IMPORT, then SAVE AS NEW and RENAME, then EXPORT.

    A project's icon

    A project can wear a little pixel icon, shown beside its name in PROJECT ▾. It has none until you pick one.

    • Pick it when you name the project: the first SAVE, SAVE AS NEW or RENAME. Tap ICON (it says NONE until there's one), or press ▼ and then SELECT.
    • The grid of icons answers the big knob or the arrows, and SELECT picks one. NO ICON takes it off, and BACK leaves it as it was.
    • It travels: a bundle carries it to another ravving, and an exported track's cover wears it in its sidebar or label block.

    Undo

    The header's ↶ ↷ step the whole project back and forth. A change that touched several machines at once counts as one step: CLEAR ALL, or a key change that moved both the bass and the lead.

    A machine's own ↶ ↷, in its toolbar, undo only that machine's changes. BACK on its grid does the same as ↶, which shows pressed. In STEP entry the cursor goes back to the column undone, ready to write it again.

    What comes back:

    • Patterns and slots.
    • The arrangement: a removed section with its content, a rename, a colour, a length, and edits to the song.

    What doesn't: sounds, the mix and the project's settings. Picking a section isn't a step either. More in Undo and redo.

    LOCK: keep a stray tap from changing what plays

    LOCK is the first button of every pattern toolbar. While it's on, the note grids don't write anything. Taps on cells, step keys, STEP entry, SELECT's moves and the note knobs are all refused, and LOCK flashes to tell you why. You can still scroll, page and listen.

    • PLAY turns LOCK on. STOP turns it off again.
    • Tap LOCK while playing to edit anyway. It then stays unlocked after you stop.
    • Tap LOCK while stopped to lock the grids. It then stays locked.

    There's one LOCK for every page, and a take (●) still records.

    Headbang all you want: while it plays, LOCK keeps your beat safe.

    SELECT: move notes after you've written them

    SELECT sits in the pattern toolbar of the bass, lead and chords, beside ♭♯. While it's on, a tap on the grid picks a note instead of writing one. Tap again to unpick it. A tap on a held note's tail picks that note.

    • ▲ ▼ move every picked note one note of the key up or down. With ♭♯ on, they move a semitone. Length, accent and slide come along.
    • Each move is one undo step.
    • On the chords page, the CHORDS view picks chords and moves them a degree. The NOTES view picks single notes. A held chord always moves as one.
    • A long press on SELECT picks every note (or chord) in all the bars, so you can move the whole pattern at once.
    • Turning SELECT off drops the picks.

    Shape a single note

    On the bass and lead, pick notes with SELECT, then turn knobs 1–3 to change them together. A readout at the top right of the grid shows the first picked note's values, with the knob badges.

    Knob Setting Range One detent
    1 LENGTH (the lighter cells) 1 step to 4 bars a step
    2 VELOCITY 1–127 4
    3 NUDGE, from where the swing puts the note half a step early to half a step late a 16th of a step
    • A long press on a cell also sets a note's length.
    • The ACCENT lane's cell fills to the velocity, bright red when accented. A tap there still flips the accent: full, or back to the plain velocity.
    • A LIVE take with Q off records a nudge for each hit.
    • These settings move with the note, each detent is one undo step, and they're kept with the project and in a saved pattern preset.

    The big knob stays the volume, and a focused group of dials keeps its own knobs.

    SLOTS: write with whole chords

    On the chords page, SLOTS lets you compose chord by chord:

    1. Tap a slot.
    2. Play a chord key. The next slot arms itself.
    3. Set each slot's length: 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars. All slots together never pass the pattern's eight bars; the "n / 8 BARS" readout keeps count.

    The slots write the chord pattern, and PLAY plays them in order with the current slot lit. ＋ adds four more slots, up to eight, and − takes them away.

    If you change the pattern in the CHORDS or NOTES view, the view says PATTERN CHANGED and dims the slots. Editing a slot then rebuilds the pattern from the slots. Undo brings your edits back.

    The header and the MIXER

    The header across the top is shared by every page. ▲ from a page's strip lands on it, ◀ ▶ walk its groups, and ▼ or BACK go back to the page.

    Key and scale

    ROOT and SCALE, at the left, set the project's key. Every melodic machine follows them: the bass, the lead and the chords play in that key, and a key change moves their lines with it. Notes from outside the scale have their own setting (Notes outside the scale, below).

    Tempo and swing

    • BPM: the tempo. With the TEMPO group focused, knob 1 sets it. When another device clocks ravving, it shows that device's tempo and can't be changed here.
    • SWING: knob 2, from 50 % (straight) to 75 % (the heaviest shuffle). Tap its caption to swing the 8ths (SWING 8, for jazz) or the 16ths (SWING 16, for house and hip hop).

    Templates and demos set both when they start a project.

    LIVE FX

    Pads that change the whole mix while you play, in two banks of sixteen: MAIN and MORE.

    • The pads: a bank's first eight sit in the header. The other eight take the instruments' place while you hold ALT, with the banks' names beside them (not while the section keys are in the header: ALT + the step keys pick sections then).
    • The bank: hold ALT and tap its name, or press ALT + ▼ for the next one.
    • The keys: hold ALT and press a step key, 1–8 for the header's pads, 9–16 for the other eight. A pad shows pressed while its key is down.
    • Your own MAIN: MENU → SETTINGS → LIVE FX picks which pads make up MAIN: the eight in the header and the eight that show with ALT. Every pad left out is in MORE.

    MAIN

    Pad What it does
    DROP While held, the kick cuts out and a high-pass sweeps up. Both come back on release.
    NO KICK, ONLY KICK Silence every kick, or everything but the kicks. One switches the other off.
    CRUSH The mix through a bit crusher.
    LO PASS, HI PASS A filter sweep: down to 300 Hz, or up to 950 Hz.
    STUTTER While held, the current 16th repeats.
    LOOP 4, LOOP 8 While held, the current beat or 8th repeats.
    KILL LOW, KILL MID, KILL HIGH Cut the lows (below 250 Hz), the mids or the highs (above 2.4 kHz), as a DJ mixer's isolator does.
    REVERSE While held, every beat plays the beat before it backwards.
    STOP While held, the tape slows to a stop over a beat.
    ECHO While held, the mix is thrown into an echo (a dotted 8th); let go and it rings out.
    FREEZE While held, a reverb takes in a moment of the mix and holds it.

    MORE

    Pad What it does
    LOOP 8T, LOOP 16, LOOP 32 While held, an 8th triplet, a 16th or a 32nd repeats.
    REWIND While held, the tape slows, stops and spins backwards faster and faster, through the last few seconds, then goes quiet.
    HALF While held, the mix plays at half speed, an octave down.
    OCT UP While held, the mix plays an octave up.
    WOBBLE While held, the pitch wavers, four times a second.
    FADE OUT While held, the mix fades to silence over two beats; let go and it is back.
    FLANGER A flanger swept once a bar.
    GATE The mix chopped into 16ths: open for the first half of each.
    AUTO-PAN The mix swings from side to side once a beat.
    MONO Both sides the same.
    PHONE A telephone's band, driven.
    ALIAS The mix held to 6 kHz, rough and aliased.

    How a pad acts

    • A tap switches it on, another off: NO KICK, ONLY KICK, LO PASS, HI PASS and the KILLs.
    • While held, and it stays on after half a second: DROP, CRUSH, FLANGER, GATE, AUTO-PAN, MONO, PHONE and ALIAS. The next tap lets it go.
    • Only while held: the rest.
    • Dragging on CRUSH or STUTTER changes how much: fewer bits, or a shorter repeat.
    • One at a time: one LOOP plays at a time, and STUTTER wins over a held LOOP. The same goes for REVERSE, REWIND, STOP, HALF, OCT UP and WOBBLE.
    • In a break: a LOOP or STUTTER pressed while nothing plays waits, and grabs the music when it comes back, from the step it lands on.
    • Every switch is smoothed. The loops, the gate, the flanger and the auto-pan follow the tempo and the bar.

    PHASER, ECHO and HEAVY sit beside the pads. Hold ALT and turn knobs 1–3 to set them from anywhere. Each shows its setting under it: how deep the phaser goes, the echo send in dB, and HEAVY's compression ratio.

    Record the whole mix

    ALT + ● records everything the speakers play, from any page, as a WAV among your recordings. The RECORD light turns red. Press ALT + ● again to stop. You'll find the file in the music player.

    Only one recording runs at a time: while this one runs, the RECORDER can't take.

    The MIXER

    ✱ opens the MIXER: every instrument at once, each on its own strip with a meter. Press ✱ or BACK to go back, or a number key for that page.

    • Pick a strip with ◀ ▶ or a step key. The step lights show the strips: muted ones dim, the picked one white.
    • Knob 1 sets its level, knob 2 its pan, and the big knob a fine level.
    • SELECT mutes it, and ALT + SELECT solos it.
    • A tap on a nameplate opens that instrument's page.

    SLOT SAMPLER

    Page 5 holds two samplers. Press 5 again to swap between SLOT SAMPLER and the PAD SAMPLER; ravving remembers which one you used.

    SLOT SAMPLER gives you four banks (A to D) of eight slots, and each bank has its own pattern. The bank on screen is the one that plays.

    Get a sound in

    Pick a slot (its row in the grid) and tap LOAD to choose one of your samples.

    Or record one: ● REC records the device's input straight into the slot, and ● again stops. The take lands in the slot, trimmed to its last burst of sound. INPUT sets up what's plugged in, the gain and the monitor.

    Shape it

    • Drag the start and end handles on the waveform. Once the waveform has the focus, knobs 1 and 2 move them too.
    • Set PITCH up to 12 semitones up or down.
    • Flip REVERSE to play it backwards.

    A slot plays one-shot: hitting it again cuts the last hit off.

    Chop and play it

    • Chop it. CHOP and AUTO CHOP open the wave editor and spread the pieces over a bank's slots (see the wave editor).
    • Play it. Each row of the step grid is a slot, with an accent row underneath. Knobs 1–3 set the selected row's mute, solo and level, just like the drums.
    • Play it live. Turn LIVE on and step keys 1–8 play the slots, with key 9 held for accents. A MIDI keyboard plays them too.

    The PAD SAMPLER

    Sixteen pads in four banks (A to D), laid out like an MPC. Pick a pad on screen and its sample, its tools and its settings show up beside the pads; its 16 steps sit underneath.

    Get a sound in

    LOAD picks a sample for the pad. ● REC records the input into it. With SMART on, the take stops by itself once the sound goes quiet. Either way, the pad gets it trimmed to its last burst.

    • COPY copies a pad onto another.
    • CLEAR empties it.
    • ERASE removes the pad's hits from the pattern.

    How a pad plays

    • NOTE ON plays the pad only while you hold it. With it off, a tap plays the whole sample (one-shot).
    • LOOP loops it from the loop point.
    • REVERSE plays it backwards.
    • MUTE silences it, and UNMUTE ALL brings every pad back.

    Tweak a pad

    The tabs put three settings each on knobs 1–3, and ◀ ▶ switch tabs:

    Tab Settings
    TRIM start and end
    MIX volume and pan
    AMP ENV attack and decay
    TUNE semitones, then fine
    PLAY mono or poly, and a mute group
    FILTER type, cutoff and resonance

    Play the pads live

    Turn LIVE on and the 16 step keys become the 16 pads. A tap plays a pad. Hold one for half a second and it latches, looping until you tap it again. STOP ends every latched loop.

    PAD FX: while a pad plays, hold ECHO, TAPE STOP, SCRATCH, STUTTER, SKIP or REVERSE to throw an effect on it. Let go and it's back to normal.

    16 LEVELS

    Switch 16 LVL on and all sixteen pads play the pad you had selected, spread by TYPE:

    • VELOCITY: soft to hard.
    • TUNE: a semitone per pad.
    • SCALE: up your project's scale.

    The kit's MODS set the kit's volume and tuning for every pad.

    The wave editor

    Both samplers share it. CHOP cuts a sample into pieces three ways:

    • REGIONS: equal slices.
    • THRESHOLD: at the hits louder than a level.
    • MANUAL: tap the wave to add a cut, and tap a cut to remove it.

    AUTO CHOP opens the editor straight on the hits. In the editor:

    • ◀ ▶ change the chop type, and the big knob sets the slices or the threshold.
    • NORMALISE raises the sample to full level.
    • TRIM cuts the silence at both ends.
    • APPLY sends the pieces to a bank's slots or to the pads.

    Undo and redo

    The project has one history. The header's ↶ ↷ walk it back and forth, and a toast names each step ("UNDO · CHORDS + BASS"). A machine's own ↶ ↷ on its pattern toolbar undo that machine's last change only, wherever it is in the history.

    • What is undone: every machine's notes, steps, chords, slots, accents, slides, lengths, automation and live timing, and the arrangement: which sections there are, their order, names, colours and lengths, and the song. A section removed comes back whole, in its place.
    • What is not: the sounds, the mix, the effects and the project's settings (tempo, swing, key). Picking a section is not a step either.
    • One step: a change that touches several machines at once is one step, like CLEAR ALL or a key change that moves the bass and the lead. So are quick changes in a row (a knob turning, a handle dragged) and a whole recorded take.
    • The history keeps the last 100 steps, and starts afresh when a project is opened or a new one begins.

    Sound sources

    The bass, lead and chords don't have to sound like their own synth. Tap the nameplate to open the machine's sounds:

    • Its own engine first: the 303 on BASS, Calf Monosynth on LEAD, TAL NoiseMaker and its factory sounds on CHORDS.
    • Then soundfont instruments, played by FluidSynth from the device's own soundfonts: an upright and other basses, Rhodes, vibes, organ, strings, sax, flute and more.

    Picking one swaps the engine for the other. A soundfont sound has MODS of its own: FILTER, ENVELOPE and MOTION. Your choice is saved with the project.

    Acoustic sounds for jazz and friends

    Machine Sounds
    CHORDS Grand piano (Fazioli), Upright piano (Kawai), Jazz guitar
    LEAD Alto sax, Tenor sax, Grand piano
    BASS Upright

    The drum machines' kit picker has an ACOUSTIC group too. These are General MIDI kits from the FluidR3 soundfont: Jazz and Brush (with the ride and the hi-hat foot on their rows, or with the crash), Standard and Room.

    Two more things help jazz feel right:

    • A bass or lead note can last longer than a step. Long-press a cell to stretch the row's note up to it.
    • Tap the header's swing caption to swing the 8ths (SWING 8) or the 16ths (SWING 16).

    Pads and textures on CHORDS

    CHORDS also has the image's pad soundfonts, grouped by name in its picker:

    • Strings: Ensemble, Fat, Digital, Rise, Bowed, Piano.
    • Organ: Phaser, Pipe 16+8+4, House, Extreme.
    • Alien, from the "Ambients & Drones" fonts: The Thing, Strange voices, Crystal voice, Glassy, Winds, Chaos, The Pulse.

    They suit a sustained, polyphonic machine: the chords hold while the voicing lasts, and the key keeps them in tune with the bass and lead.

    The sound pickers group their lists with GROUP chips, ★ for your favourites (the star, or ALT + SELECT) and SORT, as listed or A–Z. They remember the group and the sort per instrument.

    The rows of chips fold away under their FILTERS heading, which gives the list the room. Folded, the heading shows what they are set to, and the picker remembers whether they were open.

    Pattern presets, with their sound

    Open PRESET on a pattern toolbar. SAVE CURRENT AS NEW keeps the pattern together with the machine's sound: its source or factory sound, and its MODS. On the drums it keeps the kit and the kit's MODS.

    Every pack brings its own groove, and the sound it was written for.

    When you save, two switches decide what goes in. Both are on each time the dialog opens:

    • WITH ITS SOUND: the source or kit that's playing.
    • WITH ITS MODS: the MODS you've set. A knob nobody turned is left out.

    With both off, only the pattern is saved.

    A preset that carries a sound shows ♪ and the sound's name under its genre, plus "+ MODS" when it keeps MODS.

    Loading a preset

    Three switches at the top of the presets dialog decide what a preset brings along:

    • WITH ITS SOUND (off by default) switches the machine to the preset's sound.
    • WITH ITS MODS (off by default) sets the preset's MODS, only the parts it names. A preset without MODS leaves yours alone.
    • WITH ITS BARS (on by default) brings the preset's own bar count. Off, the preset is laid over your bars from the first one: a shorter preset repeats, a longer one is cut.

    Turning SOUND on turns MODS on too, since a sound is heard with its MODS. You can then switch MODS off again. MODS on alone brings a preset's MODS to the sound already playing. With all of them off, only the pattern loads. Your choices are remembered.

    SOUND and MODS follow the highlighted preset: one with no sound dims WITH ITS SOUND, and one with no MODS dims WITH ITS MODS. They light up again on a preset that has them.

    What a preset's sound keeps

    If the device doesn't have a preset's source or kit, that part of the sound stays as it is. The samplers' presets keep no sound, because their sound is in their banks. The presets that come with ravving keep no sound either. A preset copied to a stick carries its sound in its file.

    Finding presets

    The presets dialog works like the sound pickers:

    • A GROUP chip per genre, and SORT: as listed or A–Z.
    • ★ favourites: long-press a preset, tap the ★ in its corner, or press ALT + SELECT. Favourites are kept per instrument by the preset's name, so a stick's preset and the same one copied to the device share their ★.
    • FILTERS folds the SOURCE, LOAD, GROUP and SORT rows away, as in the sound pickers.
    • On a stick, SAVE CURRENT AS NEW writes the pattern there, DELETE removes the stick's file (tap it twice), and SEND… also offers THIS DEVICE, which copies the preset in.

    The dialog opens on ALL when the group you last viewed would hide the preset on screen.

    Templates

    A template is how a project starts:

    • its tempo, swing, key (root and scale) and LIVE FX knobs (PHASER, ECHO, HEAVY);
    • its sections, by name and length, in the song's order, all of them empty;
    • each instrument's sound: the engine or soundfont, its MODS, and each drum machine's kit.

    The patterns never go in a template: every one starts empty.

    1. Open PROJECT ▾ → TEMPLATES, beside NEW.
    2. Pick SOURCE: the device, or a USB stick (read straight from its ravving/presets/templates/ folder).
    3. Choose a template. It starts a new project with those settings. With unsaved changes, it asks first, just like NEW.

    SAVE TEMPLATE keeps your current session's settings, sections and sounds as a template on the device, and DELETE removes one.

    When you save a new project (the first SAVE, or SAVE AS NEW), turn on ALSO AS TEMPLATE next to ICON to keep it as a template of the same name as well.

    The USB dialog's PRESETS copies templates both ways. Every genre pack comes with a template, and some packs have one for each key.

    A new project starts clean

    NEW, with or without a template, puts every page back to its defaults:

    • an empty pattern on each machine, with no preset loaded;
    • each machine's own engine with the default sound, mix, MODS and SOUND EFFECTS;
    • tempo 120, swing straight, key C Natural Minor;
    • LIVE, STEP, AUTO and LOCK off;
    • one section, "A";
    • the LIVE FX at rest.

    A template adds its tempo, swing, key, LIVE FX knobs, sections and sounds on top. A sound that isn't on your device is skipped, and that instrument keeps its default. The demo patterns only come back through a project or a preset.

    Demos

    ravving comes with a few demo projects, in PROJECT ▾ → DEMOS. They use only the image's own soundfonts, so they sound the same on any MIKO.

    Open a demo like any project. SAVE keeps your changes as your own project under the demo's name; the demo itself never changes and can't be deleted.

    Sections and the song

    A song is more than one loop. A section holds every machine's pattern at once (an intro, a verse, a bridge) and which machines rest in it. The sounds, effects, levels, tempo and key belong to the whole project, so changing section never restarts an engine.

    Sections are live: the one on screen is the one you edit, and your edits stay in it when you pick another. You can have up to 16, and they're saved with the project.

    Pick a section

    • X and Y, the two keys beside the big knob, go to the previous and next section from any page or dialog, wrapping round. Press again before the switch lands to move the pick further instead of queueing another. With only one section, a message tells you to add more on the SONG page.
    • The SONG page (below) has a key for every section.

    While the music plays, a pick takes over on the next bar line, exactly on time: no note drops or doubles, and machines whose part doesn't change just keep playing. When stopped, a pick is immediate.

    When a switch lands: BAR or SECTION

    The SONG page's SWITCH setting is saved with the project:

    • BAR: a section you pick takes over on the next bar.
    • SECTION: it waits for the section playing to finish its pass, then switches on that bar line. Its key blinks meanwhile, and the playing one stays lit. Pick the waiting one again to cancel; stop to switch at once. A one-bar section ends every bar.

    A switch always lands on a bar line, never mid-bar.

    Queue up sections

    In LOOP you can line up to three picks. Each plays one pass, then the next takes over, and the last one keeps looping.

    • The keys show their place in the queue: 1 blinking means next.
    • Pick a queued key again to take it out of the queue.
    • Pick the playing section to queue it again after the others: with 4 playing, 2, 3 and then 4 play in that order.
    • A fourth pick is refused with QUEUE FULL.
    • Stopping puts the first one in place and drops the rest.

    In SONG, one pick waits at a time, and a new pick replaces it.

    LOOP or ONCE for each machine

    Each machine's strip has a LOOP | ONCE button beside ❚❚, saved per section like ❚❚.

    A section lasts as long as its longest pattern, or as long as you set its LENGTH. A shorter pattern LOOPs through it by default: two bars of drums play twice under a four-bar lead. On ONCE, it plays its own bars once and rests until the section starts again.

    The rest starts exactly on the bar line, and a ringing note finishes naturally. While it rests, no playhead shows, and a hit you play live is heard but not written.

    See where you are

    • The plate: the header's project plate shows the section on screen as a square key with its number, in its colour. It blinks while it waits for the bar line. Tap it to open the SONG page.
    • When a section changes, the plate sweeps in its colour and the instrument page glows round its frame, once.
    • The section keys fill up with the section's progress, here and on the SONG page.

    Section keys in the header

    Long-press the project plate, or press ALT + MODE, to swap the LIVE FX pads in the header for the section keys. Do it again to bring the pads back. MODE lights orange while ALT is held; MODE alone still cycles the entry mode.

    • Tap a key to pick it (on the next bar while playing). ▶ marks the one sounding, and its progress fills it.
    • Swipe a key up to remove that section. It follows your finger and flies off past half its height. The last section can't be removed.
    • Swipe sideways when there are more than fit. The strip also slides to the section that becomes current, or that the song reaches.
    • ＋ stays pinned at the end of the strip.
    • ALT + a step key picks section 1–16 while the section keys are there. Holding ALT leaves the instruments in place: the LIVE FX pads 9–16 show only with the pads in the header.

    With the keys in the header, ALT + 1–16 pick sections 1–16. While the header's section group has the focus (◀ ▶ onto it), the step keys alone pick them.

    The SONG page

    Open it with ALT + 1 from anywhere, and press it again to go back. It sits under the header, like the MIXER. A number key also leaves it: the page you came from comes back as you left it, on the same drum machine.

    The sections are square keys with their number and name, eight to a row. The first row matches the ALT keys. Each key has a dot for every machine that sounds in it.

    • Tap picks a section, double tap renames it, hold and drag moves it.
    • × deletes it, after a second tap.
    • ＋ copies the section on screen (A becomes A′).

    A new section, empty (NEW) or copied (＋), joins the end of the song and becomes the one on screen.

    The row above the sections has RENAME, NEW (an empty section), COLOUR, LENGTH (1–8 bars, or AUTO for the longest pattern), MOVE and ADD TO SONG.

    The song is a strip of blocks, each as wide as its bars times its repeats. Pick a block to move it, set its repeats (×1 to ×16) or remove it.

    Key On the SONG page
    ◀ ▶ Walk the keys or blocks
    ▲ ▼ Move between the section keys and the song
    SELECT or a step key Pick
    ALT + ◀ ▶ Move the picked key or block
    Knob 1 Length
    Knob 2 Repeats
    BACK Leave

    Play the whole song

    LOOP | SONG on the SONG page sets what PLAY plays: the section on screen, looped, or the song from start to end.

    • In SONG, each part is ready on time, and the song stops on the bar line after its end. The playing block shows its progress, and the plate shows the part, like "6/9".
    • The instrument pages follow the song. Their note grids are locked while it plays, as always while music plays (see LOCK). Unlock them to edit, and your edit goes into the section playing.
    • ▶ PLAY FROM HERE starts the song at any block.
    • Picking a section by hand while the song plays jumps to that section's first part. A section the song doesn't use switches back to LOOP.

    To record it all, EXPORT → WHAT: SONG records the whole song in one take, plus its tail. LOOP records BARS × LOOPS instead.

    Fixed knobs

    On a device whose top three knobs are fixed pots, a knob rarely sits where the value it takes over is. SETTINGS → DEVICE → FIXED KNOBS picks what happens when you turn it:

    Way What it does
    PICK UP The value doesn't move until the knob reaches it (or passes it), then follows the knob. Nothing jumps. The default.
    JUMP The value goes straight to where the knob is. Quick, but it can jump.
    SCALE The value moves toward the knob as you turn and meets it at the end of the knob's travel. No waiting, no jump.

    On a device with endless knobs the setting is greyed out: those never need it.

    Exports and the music player

    PROJECT ▾ → EXPORT saves your project out of the device, into the library's exports/ folder. There are two kinds.

    A track

    The whole production as the speakers hear it. ravving plays the project from its first beat for BARS × LOOPS bars, plus a TAIL for the echoes, and records it cut to the sample.

    • Formats: WAV 24-bit, FLAC, OGG Vorbis or MP3.
    • Tags: title, artist, album or mixtape, genre, composer, comment, date and BPM.
    • A cover: a night-city artwork picked at random, often with a sticker. Tap it in the dialog for another.
    • Most covers have a black sidebar: your title, one big word a line. Under it, the credits in small print: who made it, what plays, the tempo, the key, the date.
    • The others set the title on the art, beside a CD-style label block with the icon and RAVV.
    • RANDOM, SIDEBAR or CORNER under the cover picks the look. RANDOM leaves it to chance.
    • A JSON file beside it with the same details.

    The master's level is kept as it is. A track that would go over full scale is pulled down to −0.1 dBFS, and the JSON file says so.

    The ARTIST and COMPOSER start from your profile's author (MENU → SETTINGS → PROFILE: USER and AUTHOR). The ALBUM and GENRE you typed last are offered again.

    A mixtape

    Want a few tracks to go together? Give them the same ALBUM / MIXTAPE. EXPORT keeps it for next time, the MUSIC PLAYER shows them as one album under ALBUMS (key 2), and SEND… on each track takes it to a stick, a computer or your phone.

    A bundle

    A .ravv zip of the project with every sample it uses, so another ravving plays it exactly the same. PROJECT ▾ → IMPORT lists the bundles in exports/, and in the library's main folder where transfers land, and turns one into a project, with its icon.

    Notes outside the scale

    MENU → SETTINGS → DEVICE has NOTES OUTSIDE THE SCALE, on by default. It decides what happens when you play a note outside your key, for example on a MIDI keyboard, on the lead, the bass or the chords' NOTES:

    • On: the note sounds and is recorded, in its own row on the piano roll.
    • Off: it's muted there and never recorded.

    The drums, the samplers and chords played by degree ignore this setting.

    Video recordings and the computer link

    The control center's LIVE REC card records your session.

    Start a recording

    1. Tap LIVE REC. A dialog asks WHAT to record:
      • VIDEO: the screen or a USB camera with the master's sound, saved as an MP4 in the library's videos/ folder.
      • AUDIO ONLY: just the sound, a 24-bit WAV among your TRACKS in the exports folder.
    2. Choose whether the MIC is in.
    3. Tap START. The dialog is the confirmation, so a stray tap never starts a recording.

    While LIVE REC is on, the device's RECORD key starts a recording and STOP ends it, from any page. The music keeps playing. A new project switches LIVE REC off.

    Recording settings

    SETTINGS → LIVE REC has:

    • QUALITY: three levels, none of which costs the sound anything.
    • TIMER: a countdown before a recording starts.
    • SOURCE: the screen or a USB camera.
    • MIC: puts the RECORDER's input in the video's sound.
    • KEYS: names each key you press at the bottom of the video.
    • VOICE: the voice-over mix, below.

    They also apply to a recording that's already running.

    Talking over the music

    With the MIC in, ravving mixes your voice over the music:

    Sing along: the music ducks under your voice and comes back when you stop.

    • While you talk, the music ducks under your voice: DUCK −6, −12 or −18 dB, −12 by default.
    • Your voice is lifted by +6 or +12 dB and made clearer, with a low cut and a presence lift.
    • ravving tells your voice apart from the speakers leaking into the mic, so the beat doesn't duck itself.
    • A limiter keeps the mix from clipping.

    The mic's LEVEL is shown from the moment VIDEO is on, in SETTINGS and on the project plate. MIC TOO HOT means its peaks pass −6 dBFS: turn the RECORDER's GAIN down. With the RECORDER on GUITAR or LINE, the input is mixed in as it is.

    When a recording ends

    VIDEO SAVED or LIVE REC SAVED offers:

    • DELETE, on the left. A second tap within three seconds confirms.
    • RENAME. The file keeps its extension.
    • SEND… to a USB stick, one of your computers or your phone.
    • METADATA… for an audio recording. It opens the export dialog over the file to tag it, draw its cover and save it as WAV, FLAC, OGG or MP3.

    The same SEND… is on the music player's tracks, in FILES and in the presets dialogs. The export dialog can also send a new track or bundle to a computer as it's written.

    Your computers

    SETTINGS → CONNECTIONS → COMPUTERS holds any number of laptops or PCs on your network.

    1. Tap ADD A COMPUTER.
    2. Enter its host, your user name and your password. The password is used once, to install the device's key on that computer.
    3. Files you send land in that computer's Downloads folder.

    Each computer has CHANGE (set it up again), TEST and FORGET.

    Sending to a phone

    Every SEND… button has a third destination, beside the USB sticks and the computers: PHONE · QR CODE. You'll find SEND… in FILES, VIDEO SAVED, the music player, the presets dialogs and the sound picker. It works with any phone, iPhone included, with no pairing, app or account.

    1. Make sure the phone is on the same Wi-Fi as the device. The dialog tells you if the device isn't on a network.
    2. Tap PHONE · QR CODE. A QR code appears.
    3. Point the phone's camera at it and open the link.
    4. Safari or Chrome saves the file to the phone's Downloads (Files on an iPhone).

    The dialog shows WAITING FOR THE PHONE, then SENDING n %, then DOWNLOADED ✓, and a message tells you when the whole file has arrived. The link is also shown in words, if you'd rather type it.

    • The link works while the dialog is open, for up to 15 minutes. Closing the dialog ends it.
    • It sends one file at a time. Given several, it sends the first and says so.

    The ravving page on your phone

    The same server also carries the ravving page: the instructions, the features and every shortcut. The device has no browser of its own, so SETTINGS → HELP & ABOUT → OPEN shows the page as a QR code for a phone, or an address for a laptop.

    The QR code points at the public page, https://ravving.github.io/, which works from anywhere. The device's own address sits under it (http://<device>:8098/help/), for a Wi-Fi without internet.

    Files

    The control center's FILES card shows everything in your library by kind, newest first: projects, templates, presets, samples, takes, the RECORDER's takes, recordings, tracks, bundles, soundfonts, MIDI files, videos and received files.

    Each file has SEND… (a USB stick, one of your computers, or your phone) and DELETE. FILES is the one place where files leave the device or get deleted. The PROJECT dialog opens, saves and exports, and the USB page brings a stick's files in.

    Browse from your phone

    PHONE · BROWSE, at the top of FILES, shows a QR code that opens your library as web pages on a phone or laptop on the same Wi-Fi:

    • every kind, with how many files it has;
    • each kind's files, with their details, size and date;
    • a DOWNLOAD button for each file.

    Projects, and anything else that is a folder, are listed as "on the device". The link stays open until 15 minutes pass without the phone asking for anything, even with the dialog closed. After that, the phone says the link has run out.

    USB sticks

    Pattern presets from a stick

    Open a pattern toolbar's PRESET. With a stick in, a SOURCE row appears: DEVICE, or USB · and the stick's name. Pick the stick to see its presets for that machine and play one straight from there, without copying it. One undo step puts your pattern back.

    Soundfonts from a stick

    A soundfont you import works right away:

    • If it looks like a drum kit, it joins the kit picker under IMPORTED.
    • Every preset in it becomes a sound for the chords, bass and lead, listed as the font's name · the preset's name, and grouped by font.

    More than one stick, or a locked one

    With several sticks or partitions plugged in, the USB card names each one and asks which to open.

    A read-only stick (a locked stick, or an installer image) says so. Nothing is copied to it, and its page offers FORMAT. FORMAT erases the whole stick into one writable FAT32 partition called RAVVING. EJECT and FORMAT both need a second press within three seconds.

    Bluetooth

    MENU → BLUETOOTH lists your devices: the connected ones first, then the paired ones, then the ones in range. A paired device you aren't connected to reads "paired · not connected".

    Find and pair a device

    1. Switch Bluetooth on.
    2. Tap SCAN. It's a short scan for speakers, headphones, phones and computers.
    3. Tap the device to open its row, then tap PAIR.
    4. Confirm the passkey, or type the PIN, when ravving asks at the top.

    SCAN doesn't start on its own while a speaker plays, because a scan makes a Bluetooth sound stutter on the device's shared radio. VISIBLE lets a phone find the device for three minutes, so it can send you a file.

    What a device's row offers

    • CONNECT / DISCONNECT.
    • SEND… sends an exported track or bundle; the progress runs along the bottom.
    • PLAY THROUGH sends the whole studio's sound to a speaker or headphones. DEVICE OUTPUT brings it back. The same choice is in the drawer's AUDIO OUTPUT card, with the jacks and HDMI.
    • SBC-XQ picks the sound quality for this device: on for SBC-XQ, finer sound with more data; off for plain SBC, sturdier on a weak link. Devices you haven't set follow SETTINGS. If a speaker sounds crunchy on SBC-XQ, switch it off for that speaker.
    • FORGET unpairs it.

    Files a phone sends you land in the library's incoming/ folder. A bundle shows up in PROJECT ▾ → IMPORT, and a track in the music player under "Received".

    When a device doesn't connect

    A speaker that's asleep, waking up or busy with a phone gets called again three more times over about 35 seconds. Its row reads "waking…" meanwhile, and DISCONNECT stops trying. If it still doesn't answer, ravving says NOT ANSWERING: FREE IT FROM A PHONE, OR TURN IT OFF AND ON.

    Speaker dozing off? ravving calls it three more times before giving up.

    One audio device at a time

    Only one speaker or pair of headphones plays at a time. Connecting a second one while another plays hands the sound over to it, and the first one disconnects.

    When Bluetooth comes on, ravving reconnects the output you picked last, once, if nothing else is playing.

    If a Bluetooth output goes silent while music is sent to it, ravving routes the sound to it again by itself. If that doesn't bring the sound back, the issues center tells you.

    Zynthbox, as it ships on MIKO, needs a little setup for Bluetooth, and ravving's setup takes care of it.

    Audio output

    MENU → AUDIO OUTPUT picks where the studio's sound goes:

    • the device's jacks, the default;
    • HDMI;
    • any Bluetooth speaker or headphones connected in the BLUETOOTH dialog.

    The sound comes out of one place only, without glitches. The list updates while the dialog is open, as things are plugged in. The music player follows the output you pick too.

    Your choice is remembered

    When the output goes away, for example headphones turned off, the sound falls back to the jacks. It switches back to your output as soon as it's there again, after a restart or a reconnection. For a Bluetooth output, ravving keeps its address so Bluetooth can reconnect it when it comes on. Picking the jacks forgets your choice.

    Volume and quality

    • The drawer's OUTPUT slider (knob 2) sets the level: the amplifier's on the jacks, or the output's own level otherwise.
    • The drawer's BRIGHTNESS slider (knob 1) sets the screen's brightness. A black screen is often just this one turned all the way down.
    • SBC-XQ: each paired speaker or pair of headphones has its own SBC-XQ chip in the BLUETOOTH dialog. Off plays it on plain SBC, which is sturdier on a weak link. Devices without their own choice follow SETTINGS.

    If an output goes silent while music is sent to it for six seconds, ravving routes the sound again once. If it's still silent, the issues center tells you.

    The RECORDER

    MENU → RECORDER is a full-screen page for recording what you play into the input, as you play it. The beat keeps going, so you can record a take over it.

    Set up the input

    • The input: a tab for MIC, GUITAR or LINE.
    • MONITOR and its LEVEL, so you hear yourself.
    • GAIN, with a meter, and lights for the jacks.
    • ● REC, beside a graph of the incoming level.

    KEEP TAKES IN decides where takes go:

    • SAMPLES (samples/recordings), where the samplers' browsers find them;
    • TRACKS (the library's recordings), where the music player finds them.

    A take is kept exactly as it came in: no compression, no trimming, no SMART stop.

    Effects for the input

    The EFFECTS row has effects made for the input. On GUITAR you get amps and pedals: AMP, OVERDRIVE, FUZZ, TUBE, WAH and HOT AMP.

    Your recordings

    On the right are the RECORDINGS from both places, newest first. Each one has:

    • ▶ PLAY.
    • TRIM and NORMALISE, which make a new recording beside it.
    • TO SLOT SAMPLER / TO PADS, into a slot or a pad.
    • CHOP…, onto SLOT SAMPLER's bank or the pads, through the wave editor.
    • TO TRACKS / TO SAMPLES, RENAME and DELETE (twice).

    The tuner

    On GUITAR the page has a TUNER. It listens to the input before the effects and shows the note and how many cents off it is, on a ±50 scale. The tuning's strings are buttons.

    • AUTO goes through the strings from the lowest. Hold a string within 3 cents for 0.8 s and it's ticked ✓, then the next one comes up, until ALL STRINGS IN TUNE.
    • MANUAL stays on the string you tap.
    • TUNING picks a tuning, and A4 sets the reference pitch (430–450 Hz).
    Instrument Tunings
    Guitar standard, half and full step down, drop D and C, DADGAD, open D, G, E and C
    Bass 4 and 5 strings, drop D
    Ukulele GCEA, baritone

    The tuner only runs while it's on screen.

    The issues center

    The ISSUES card in the menu, beside MUSIC PLAYER, SETTINGS and the USB card, tells you what's wrong on the device right now, in plain words, with what to do about it.

    • ALL GOOD when nothing is wrong.
    • Otherwise, the number of issues, lit in the colour of the worst one.

    Tap it for the full list, worst first. Each issue has its details, a suggestion, and a button where one helps, like RESTART RAVVING. New errors and warnings also pop up as a message.

    What it watches:

    • the sound engine missing, or an engine that couldn't start;
    • audio connections that never appeared, and audio dropouts;
    • the device's keys stopping responding;
    • the music player unable to play;
    • Bluetooth unavailable, or an output that plays no sound;
    • an export that failed, a USB stick that wouldn't mount, storage nearly full.

    Under the list are the last warnings ravving printed.

    Where your files live

    ravving keeps to its own folders: it reads the device's sample library and writes only to these places:

    Root On the device What
    config ~/.config/ravving settings (the input set-up, the UI's choices)
    state ~/.local/state/ravving the live session (session.json, the sequence); removed by a purge
    cache ~/.cache/ravving rebuildable: reversed samples, raw takes, the LV2 port cache
    library /zynthian/zynthian-my-data/ravving the user's work, kept across reinstalls: projects/, presets/, samples/ (takes in samples/takes/, the RECORDER's in samples/recordings/, imported bundles' samples in samples/imported/), recordings/ (the master's and the RECORDER's TRACKS), exports/

    On a desktop the library is ~/.local/share/ravving.
