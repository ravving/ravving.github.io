    Bluetooth

    Devices, pairing, file transfer and audio output over BlueZ and PipeWire.

    How ravving uses BlueZ and PipeWire for Bluetooth: devices, pairing, file transfer and audio output. The classes are in the API reference; the system setup is on Running on a Zynthbox.

    Ravving's Bluetooth does what a phone's does: pair, connect, send a file, play through a speaker or headphones, receive a file. What a Zynthbox needs for it is in packaging.md ("Bluetooth on a fresh device"), the tests in testing.md and the troubleshooting table in debugging.md.

    The backend

    Bluetooth (src/backend/bluetooth.*, QML bluetooth), on BluezQt. The UI is BluetoothPopup, from the control center's BLUETOOTH card.

    • The adapter: power, discovery, and discoverable for three minutes.
    • The devices: connected first, then paired, then by signal. Phones with a private address and no name stay hidden until paired.
    • Pairing: an Agent (DisplayYesNo). A passkey to confirm or a PIN to type lands in pairing, answered with answerPairing. Then pair → trust → connect, or forget.
    • Files: OBEX Object Push sending (sendFile, progress in transfer), and an ObexAgent accepting pushes into the library's incoming/ folder.
    • Audio: the switch to a Bluetooth output (useAsOutput).

    Discovery

    The Pi has one radio for Bluetooth and Wi-Fi, and an inquiry breaks up a playing A2DP link. So a scan lasts 12 s (DiscoverySeconds), and only starts on its own while no audio device is connected.

    It scans classic devices only (setDiscoveryFilter {Transport: bredr}). A dual-mode speaker paired from its LE advertisement gets no A2DP sink, and the Pi's firmware has no LE Audio.

    Audio output

    When an audio device connects, WirePlumber makes a sink node bluez_output.<address>.1, plus an input (capture) node for HFP. useAsOutput hands it to AudioOutputs (outputs, also the control center's AUDIO OUTPUT card).

    AudioOutputs doesn't make it the default sink. That would clock the whole JACK graph by the Bluetooth transport, in 256-frame chunks (glitches), and leave the master's links to the jacks in place (sound from both). Instead:

    1. The jacks keep the clock.
    2. ravving starts its own pw-loopback, ravving-out: a virtual sink whose playback stream targets the Bluetooth sink, with a 2048-frame buffer.
    3. The master's two output ports are linked to it with pw-link. Its inputs have no JACK name, so libzynthbox's JackConnectionHandler can't.
    4. The jacks are muted, not unlinked. An unlinked jacks sink would suspend, and the Bluetooth transport would become the graph's clock.

    Off, on disconnect, forget or exit, the loopback stops and the jack links come back. A device connected on the headset profile only (playback_MONO) gets a mono mix-down.

    The codec

    PipeWire offers a2dp-sink-sbc_xq beside plain SBC, since ravving's WirePlumber drop-in sets bluez5.enable-sbc-xq. But WirePlumber connects with plain SBC.

    • SETTINGS · BLUETOOTH · SBC-XQ (AudioOutputs.preferSbcXq, on by default) switches the output in use to SBC-XQ when it offers it, or back to plain SBC. That happens once per connection (wpctl set-profile).
    • Each paired device can override it with its own chip in the BLUETOOTH dialog (AudioOutputs.sbcXqByDevice, setSbcXqFor()). A device may advertise the bitpool and still not sustain it.
    • A codec switch replaces the sink node under the same name. AudioOutputs notices the node id change and routes to the new one. A Bluetooth output gets its codec first, and is routed to once its new sink is there.

    The silence watch

    While another output plays, OutputWatch listens to what goes into the loopback and what comes out. If music goes in and silence comes out for six seconds, it routes again once, with a fresh loopback. If it's still silent, the issues center says the output plays no sound.

    One audio device at a time

    BlueZ keeps one local A2DP endpoint per codec, shared by every device, and a local endpoint carries one stream. PipeWire registers one SBC source, which SBC-XQ reuses. So while one device streams SBC, a second SBC device can't set up its audio (Unable to select SEP, br-connection-create-socket).

    When a device you're connecting answers but can't connect while another audio device is connected, ravving:

    1. shows SWITCHING FROM · the other;
    2. disconnects the other one;
    3. waits 1 s for BlueZ to free the endpoint, and connects again.

    ravving's own reconnect never takes the link from a playing device.

    When a device doesn't answer

    A page timeout (Host is down) is retried after 2, 5 and 10 s. With BlueZ's 5.12 s page, that's about 37 s, while the row reads "waking…". Disconnecting or forgetting stops it.

    After that, it reads COULD NOT CONNECT, with the hint NOT ANSWERING: FREE IT FROM A PHONE, OR TURN IT OFF AND ON. PLAY THROUGH's sink poll (15 s) starts once the connection is made.

    The kept output comes back

    AudioOutputs keeps the Bluetooth address of the output picked. When Bluetooth comes on, at a start or with the switch, that device is connected once, 3 s later, if it's paired and nothing else plays. Never in a loop: paging a speaker in standby wakes it up.

    Files

    • Sending opens an OBEX session (Target: opp) and pushes the file.
    • Receiving needs the device VISIBLE (discoverable and pairable, for three minutes). Files land under library/incoming/. PROJECT ▾ → IMPORT lists them as "received", and the music library as the album "Received".
